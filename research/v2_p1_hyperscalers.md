# v2 Pod P1 — Hyperscalers & Compute Capex

**Analyst pod:** v2 P1 · **Date:** 2026-07-10 · **Reads with:** v1 `research/pod4_cloud_models_apps.md` (market structure, DAU/MAU, model capability) — this memo does not repeat that ground. It extends it financially: who actually earns the AI-cloud dollar, whether legacy businesses drag or fund the buildout, how capex compares to the four US hyperscalers, and — the brief's critical question — **who is paying for it**.

---

## Scope

Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu and ByteDance/Volcengine, benchmarked against Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta on: (1) AI-cloud revenue/profit as a share of group results; (2) whether legacy segments (e-commerce, ads, gaming) are dragging or funding the AI build-out; (3) capex scale, growth and intensity 2024→2026; (4) funding source — internal operating cash flow vs. external debt/equity — and each company's capacity to sustain rising capex. Fiscal-year note: Alibaba's FY runs April–March (FY2026 = year ended 2026-03-31); Microsoft's FY runs July–June; Tencent, Baidu, ByteDance, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta report on calendar years. All comparisons below are labeled by the reporting entity's own fiscal convention — do not read "2026" as calendar-aligned across companies without checking the label.

## AI-cloud contribution: who earns it, and how much

**Alibaba** is the cleanest disclosure. Cloud Intelligence Group revenue in the March-2026 quarter (Q4 FY2026) was RMB41,626m (+38% YoY; external-customer growth accelerated to 40%), against total group revenue of RMB243,380m (+3% YoY reported, +11% like-for-like excluding disposed Sun Art/Intime) — cloud is now roughly 17% of group revenue in that quarter. Within cloud, AI-related product revenue was RMB8,971m, its 11th consecutive quarter of triple-digit YoY growth — i.e., AI product revenue alone is already ~3.7% of group revenue and the fastest-growing line in the company (B, company release/SEC 6-K) [1][2].

**Baidu** shows the sharpest AI/non-AI divergence of the four. In Q1 2026, AI Cloud Infra revenue was RMB8.8bn (+79% YoY, +52% QoQ), and Baidu's self-defined "Core AI-powered Business" reached RMB13.6bn (+49% YoY) — for the first time over half (52%) of Baidu Core (General Business) revenue. Simultaneously, Online Marketing Services (legacy search ads) fell to RMB12.6bn (-22% YoY, -17% QoQ) and "Legacy Business" revenue fell to RMB10.2bn (-29% YoY), pulling total group revenue down 2% QoQ to RMB32.1bn (B, company release) [3][4][5]. This is the plainest evidence in the sample that a **legacy segment is dragging, not funding** — AI growth is outrunning a shrinking ad base, not being bankrolled by it.

**Tencent** discloses AI/cloud only inside a blended "FinTech & Business Services" (FBS) segment, which reached RMB59.9bn in Q1 2026 (+9% YoY); within it, Business Services revenue (cloud + AI + e-commerce tech fees) grew 20% YoY on cloud/AI demand and a "more favorable pricing environment" (i.e., the 2026 price-hike cycle documented in v1). Group revenue was RMB196.5bn (+9% YoY), non-IFRS net profit RMB67.9bn (+11% YoY) (B, company release) [6][7]. Tencent does not break out a standalone AI-cloud revenue line the way Alibaba and Baidu do — this is a genuine disclosure gap, not an omission on this pod's part.

**ByteDance** (private) discloses nothing audited. Sacra's equity-research estimate puts 2025 group revenue at ~$186bn (+~20% YoY from ~$155bn in 2024), with international revenue crossing 30% of the total for the first time (D, private-company estimate — not verifiable against a filing) [8]. Multiple press reports citing sourcing (Yicai, BigGo) put 2025 net profit down more than 70% YoY specifically because of AI-capex/R&D spend (C, sourced but unaudited) [9][10]. Volcengine's own MaaS (Model-as-a-Service) revenue target — carried over from v1 — is RMB15bn for 2026 vs. ~RMB1.5bn in 2025, a 10x jump that is itself evidence AI monetization is still a rounding error against total group revenue.

**Net read:** Alibaba and Baidu now give investors a genuine AI-segment P&L line; Tencent does not (its AI contribution is inferred, not disclosed); ByteDance's AI economics are opaque estimates layered on an unaudited base. Baidu is the only one of the four where the "AI vs. legacy" contrast is a clean, company-stated number (52% of Core revenue) rather than an analyst inference.

## Non-AI drag: does e-commerce/ads/gaming fund or drag the build-out?

**Alibaba — drag, via a second, unrelated subsidy war.** China E-commerce Group (Taobao/Tmall) FY2026 revenue was RMB449,385m (+4% YoY); customer-management revenue (CMR) grew 5% YoY (7% like-for-like once a subsidy-related accounting reclassification is excluded). But adjusted EBITA for the whole e-commerce group fell 44% YoY to RMB107,509m in FY2026, driven by the instant-retail ("Taobao Instant Commerce") subsidy war layered on top of the cloud capex super-cycle (B, SEC 6-K) [1][11]. The upshot: Alibaba is fighting two capital-intensive wars simultaneously — quick-commerce subsidies and AI-cloud capex — and the e-commerce profit pool that historically funded everything else nearly halved in FY2026. Group adjusted net profit fell 99.7% YoY to RMB86m in the March quarter [1]. Free cash flow flipped from +RMB73,870m (FY2025) to **-RMB46,609m (FY2026)** — Alibaba's first FCF-negative year on record in this data set (C, aggregator summary of the SEC filing) [12].

**Baidu — drag, not fund, and explicitly acknowledged.** As shown above, legacy ads/search revenue is shrinking double-digits while AI cloud grows triple-digits; legacy is not generating surplus cash to redeploy into AI, it is being overtaken by it. Baidu's own Q1 2026 operating cash flow was RMB2.7bn — only the third consecutive positive quarter since OCF turned positive in Q3 2025 — a thin base against declared "strategic investment intensity" ambitions (B, earnings call, reproduced by secondary transcript) [5][13].

**Tencent — the exception; funds it comfortably.** Tencent's non-gaming, non-ads core (games + social + ads) remains highly profitable — Q1 2026 non-IFRS net profit of RMB67.9bn dwarfs the RMB31.9bn quarterly capex figure. Gaming and advertising are not disclosed as literally "funding" AI capex in a ring-fenced sense, but unlike Alibaba and Baidu, Tencent's legacy businesses are unambiguously cash-generative enough that capex is a rounding error against operating profit, not a competing claim on a shrinking pool (B, company release) [6][14].

**ByteDance — unknown.** TikTok/Douyin ad revenue (the presumptive "legacy" cash engine) is not separately disclosed; the >70%-YoY profit decline is consistent with AI spend outrunning ad-business cash generation, but with no audited segment data this cannot be confirmed beyond a directional inference (D) [9][10].

## Funding source — the critical question

This is where the four Chinese majors and the four US hyperscalers diverge sharply, and where the Chinese majors diverge from each other.

**Alibaba — pure internal funding is ending.** FY2025 free cash flow was solidly positive (+RMB73,870m); FY2026 FCF flipped negative (-RMB46,609m / -$6.76bn), driven jointly by cloud capex and the instant-retail subsidy war. To bridge the gap, Alibaba raised debt-like instruments in FY2026: net proceeds of RMB20,967m from convertible unsecured senior notes (plus capped-call payments), and RMB10,986m from exchangeable bonds, including a July-2025 zero-coupon exchangeable-bond issue referencing its Alibaba Health subsidiary (~HK$12bn principal) (B, SEC 6-K) [12]. Against a RMB520,824m (~$75.5bn) cash-and-investments cushion as of March-2026, these raises are modest relative to the balance sheet, but the *direction of travel* — FCF-negative, tapping capital markets — is the same signal the US hyperscalers are showing at far larger scale.

**Tencent — still self-funded, but has reopened the bond market.** Quarterly FCF (~RMB43–47bn per source, C-grade — precise quarter labeling inconsistent across the secondary sources reviewed) comfortably exceeds quarterly capex (RMB31.9bn in Q1 2026). Tencent nonetheless issued dim-sum bonds in 2026 — its first bond issuance in any currency since April 2021 — which 36Kr and other trackers characterize primarily as refinancing maturing notes ($1bn due Jan-2026, $500m due Apr-2026) and debt-structure optimization rather than new capex funding (C) [19][20]. Read this as optionality-seeking, not necessity — but it is notable that even China's most FCF-rich AI-cloud operator chose 2026 to re-enter debt markets after a five-year absence.

**Baidu — most externally-dependent of the three listed majors, on management's own words.** Operating cash flow (RMB2.7bn, Q1 2026) is a small fraction of what AI-cloud capex evidently requires. On the Q1 2026 earnings call, management stated explicitly that Baidu is "drawing on a mix of financing channels and different instruments, including operating leases, financial leases, and other low-cost bank borrowings" to fund AI investment (B — direct management statement, reproduced across multiple transcript aggregators) [5][13]. This is the clearest company-level admission in the sample that internal cash generation alone is not covering AI capex.

**ByteDance — opaque; profit compression is the only visible signal.** No public bond or credit-facility disclosure surfaced in this pod's search. The >70% YoY profit decline (2025) is consistent with capex/R&D being funded by squeezing retained earnings/net income rather than raising external capital, but this is an inference, not a disclosed fact (D) [9][10].

**US hyperscalers — the marginal capex dollar is now visibly external, led by Alphabet.** Combined, the four are on track to spend ~90% of operating cash flow on 2026 capex (up from ~65% in 2025), and are expected to issue **>$400bn in new debt in 2026**, more than double the $165bn raised in 2025 (C, analyst synthesis) [16][17]. Alphabet is the standout case: after a February-2026 multi-tranche bond sale (a $20bn dollar tranche + ~$7.5bn sterling tranche + a rare 100-year bond, on top of a $25bn November-2025 sale — long-term debt reportedly quadrupled in 2025 to $46.5bn) [21][22][23], Alphabet then priced a **$84.75bn equity offering in June 2026** (upsized from $80bn, growing to $90bn with underwriter over-allotment) — common stock, Class C stock, and mandatory convertible preferred — explicitly to fund AI infrastructure capex, confirmed via SEC 8-K/FWP filings (A/B) [24][25][26]. DataCenterDynamics estimated this debt program alone financed ~15% of Alphabet's ~$180bn 2026 capex plan [23]. Forward estimates (not company guidance — flag D) project Alphabet FCF could fall ~90% to ~$8.2bn in 2026 from $73.3bn in 2025 (Pivotal Research), and Amazon FCF could turn negative by $17–28bn in 2026 (Morgan Stanley / Bank of America) [16].

**Synthesis:** the US hyperscalers are financing their AI build-out with capital markets at a scale and speed with no Chinese analog yet — Alphabet's single June-2026 equity raise ($84.75–90bn) is roughly 5x Alibaba's *entire* FY2026 capex, and larger than ByteDance's most aggressive reported 2026 capex figure. But the *pattern* — FCF compression, a pivot toward external financing — is now visible at Alibaba and (defensively) Tencent too, and is already the stated norm at Baidu. China's AI-capex super-cycle is earlier-stage and smaller in absolute dollars, but it is following the same internal-cash-to-external-capital trajectory the US hyperscalers are running through at 10x the scale.

## Capability vs. global frontier

Not re-litigated here — v1 pod4 [see that memo, sources 13–25] covers the DeepSeek-V4-Pro / GLM-5.2 / Hunyuan Hy3 frontier-gap narrowing and the OpenRouter token-share shift in detail. This pod's contribution is that the financial capacity to keep funding that capability race is now visibly strained at three of the four majors (Alibaba, Baidu explicitly; Tencent defensively) — capability convergence and financial sustainability are not the same claim, and should not be conflated when assessing durability of the "6–9 month gap" narrative.

## Moats & unit economics

Extending v1's read: Alibaba's moat (scale + vertical integration) is real but is now being financed partly with dilutive/debt instruments rather than pure FCF, a genuine change in capital-structure risk that did not exist in the FY2025 telling of the same story. Tencent's moat is the cleanest in unit-economics terms — it is the only one of the four funding its AI build-out from a legacy cash-generation engine (games/social/ads) that is not itself in structural decline, which is why its bond issuance reads as optionality rather than necessity. Baidu's "AI is now the majority of the business" framing (52% of Core revenue) is real but sits atop a legacy ad business declining ~22–29% YoY and cash generation (RMB2.7bn OCF) an order of magnitude below what its own capex evidently requires — the company has said as much on its own earnings call. ByteDance's economics remain the least verifiable of the four; its profit-margin compression is the only hard signal available, and it comes from unaudited, sourced-not-filed reporting.

---

## Bottlenecks & dependencies

1. **Capital-structure risk is now a first-order investability variable, not a footnote.** Alibaba's FCF turned negative for the first time in this data set; this changes what "self-funding national champion" means for equity holders relative to the FY2025 telling of the same story.
2. **Disclosure asymmetry.** Alibaba and Baidu give investors a genuine AI-segment revenue line; Tencent does not (AI contribution must be inferred from a blended FBS segment); ByteDance discloses nothing audited. This materially affects how confidently a reader can underwrite any of the four's forward AI economics.
3. **Two simultaneous capital wars at Alibaba** (instant-retail subsidies + cloud capex) compound the funding-source pressure in a way none of the other three majors face — Tencent and Baidu are not fighting a comparable second subsidy war.
4. **US hyperscaler capex is decisively outscaling the Chinese majors in absolute dollars** even as growth *rates* narrow — the gap in capital-markets access (Alphabet raising $84.75–90bn in a single equity offering) has no Chinese analog at this scale, which bounds how fast the AI-cloud capacity gap can close regardless of model-capability convergence.

## Investability read

- **Alibaba's AI-cloud story is real and accelerating (38% cloud growth, 11 straight quarters of triple-digit AI-product growth) but the FY2026 FCF-negative turn is a material change in the risk profile** — investors are now underwriting a capital-structure bet (converts/exchangeables against a large cash buffer) on top of the operating bet. This was not true a year earlier in the v1 telling.
- **Tencent remains the cleanest funding-quality story of the four** — capex is comfortably covered by a legacy cash-generation engine that is not itself in decline, and its 2026 bond issuance reads as opportunistic refinancing rather than distress or necessity.
- **Baidu is the highest-conviction "AI is real" revenue story (52% of Core revenue, +79% cloud growth) paired with the weakest funding-quality picture** — management's own words confirm reliance on leases and bank borrowings, and legacy ad revenue is declining, not funding, the pivot. Read Baidu as the name where AI-cloud execution risk is lowest but capital-structure risk is highest among the three listed majors.
- **ByteDance remains uninvestable directly** and its AI-era profit/capex picture is the least verifiable of the four — treat all figures here as D-grade, sourced-not-filed.
- **US hyperscaler comparison matters for relative valuation, not just absolute scale**: Alphabet alone raised more equity capital in a single June-2026 offering ($84.75–90bn) than Alibaba's entire FY2026 capex — the AI-infrastructure capital-markets gap between the two ecosystems remains an order of magnitude, even as growth rates narrow.

## GLOSSARY

- **Capex intensity** — capital expenditure as a percentage of revenue; a measure of how much of every revenue dollar is being reinvested into infrastructure rather than flowing to profit or shareholders.
- **FCF (Free Cash Flow)** — cash generated from operations minus capital expenditure; the cash actually left over after infrastructure spend, and the key measure of whether a capex build-out is self-funding.
- **OCF (Operating Cash Flow)** — cash generated purely from core business operations, before capex is subtracted; distinct from FCF and from accounting net profit.
- **RPO (Remaining Performance Obligation)** — contracted-but-not-yet-recognized cloud/AI revenue; US hyperscalers disclose this as a forward-demand signal, Chinese cloud majors generally do not with the same granularity — a disclosure gap noted in this memo.
- **MaaS (Model-as-a-Service)** — selling access to AI models by usage (API calls/tokens) rather than software licenses; the core Chinese cloud-monetization model for foundation models (e.g., Volcengine's MaaS revenue line).
- **CMR (Customer Management Revenue)** — Alibaba's term for the commissions/ad fees it earns from merchants on Taobao/Tmall; its core e-commerce monetization metric.
- **EBITA** — earnings before interest, tax and amortization; Alibaba's preferred segment-profitability metric (adjusted EBITA), similar to but distinct from EBITDA.
- **Convertible / exchangeable bonds** — debt instruments that can convert into equity (of the issuer or, for exchangeables, of a related listed subsidiary) at a set price; a middle ground between pure debt and pure equity financing, used by Alibaba in FY2026 to help fund capex.
- **Dim-sum bonds** — offshore RMB-denominated bonds, typically issued in Hong Kong; Tencent's 2026 issuance was in this format.
- **6-K / 8-K / 10-K / 20-F** — US SEC filing types: 6-K (foreign private issuer's periodic/current report, used by Alibaba, Baidu, Tencent's ADR-adjacent disclosures), 8-K (US issuer's current report, used by Meta/Alphabet), 10-K/20-F (annual report — domestic/foreign issuer respectively).
- **ASIC (Application-Specific Integrated Circuit)** — a chip custom-designed for one workload (e.g., a China-designed AI inference chip), as opposed to general-purpose GPUs; referenced in Tencent's 2026 capex-ramp commentary.

## LIMITATIONS

- **Baidu's actual RMB capex figure could not be located within this pod's search budget.** Management confirmed the funding *mix* (leases, bank borrowings) on the Q1-2026 earnings call but no press or filing summary reviewed gave a specific quarterly or full-year capex number; this is flagged as an open data gap rather than papered over with an estimate.
- **ByteDance figures are the weakest in the memo by construction** — it is private and discloses no audited financials. Revenue ($186bn 2025), profit-decline (>70% YoY) and capex ($25bn 2025; $29.4bn–$70bn 2026, a nearly 2.5x range) all trace to press reporting citing unnamed sources or third-party equity-research estimates (Sacra).
