# Pod 3 — Compute & AI Chips (L4) + Software Ecosystems

**Analyst pod:** Pod 3 · **Layer:** L4 (AI accelerators & CUDA-equivalent software) · **As-of:** 2026-07-08

## Scope

This memo covers China's indigenous AI-accelerator landscape — Huawei Ascend, Cambricon, Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Iluvatar CoreX, Baidu Kunlunxin, Alibaba T-Head, and ByteDance's in-house silicon effort — plus the CUDA-equivalent software stacks (Huawei CANN/MindSpore, Cambricon NeuWare) that determine whether the hardware is actually usable at scale. It also covers Nvidia's shrinking China footprint (H20/H200/B30 export policy whiplash, gray-market smuggling), the domestic-chip procurement mandate for state-funded data centers, and the 2025-2026 domestic-GPU IPO wave that has turned this layer into the most conspicuous investable story in China tech.

## Market structure & value chain

L4 sits at the center of the stack: it converts SMIC/Hua Hong-fabricated silicon and CXMT/domestic HBM into usable training/inference compute for L6 clouds and L7 model labs. Three structurally distinct groups compete here:

1. **Vertically-integrated national champions** — Huawei (Ascend + CANN + CloudMatrix networking + Huawei Cloud, fully private, unlisted) and, to a lesser extent, Alibaba (T-Head/PingTouGe chips feeding Alibaba Cloud) and Baidu (Kunlunxin feeding Baidu AI Cloud and third parties). These firms design chips primarily to secure their own cloud/model roadmaps, with external sales a secondary channel.
2. **Merchant fabless GPU/NPU vendors** — Cambricon (already listed, SSE:688256), and the "GPU IPO wave" cohort: Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Iluvatar CoreX. These sell general-purpose GPGPUs into the open market (state-owned telecoms, provincial data centers, ByteDance, Geely, banks) and have just completed or are completing IPOs that turned into the most oversubscribed listings of the cycle.
3. **In-house/hyperscaler silicon** — ByteDance is developing a Groq-inspired inference ASIC (Arm/RISC-V candidate architectures, partnering with InnoStar Semiconductor on memory to reduce HBM dependence) while simultaneously remaining Nvidia's and Huawei's single largest China customer by dollar volume in 2026.

Foundry is the binding constraint for nearly everyone except the TSMC-dependent fabless names (MetaX, Iluvatar, Enflame use TSMC where still possible; Moore Threads was severed from TSMC by its October 2023 Entity List listing and now depends on SMIC). SMIC's most advanced logic node is ~7nm (N+2, DUV-based) versus TSMC's 2nm, and SMIC holds only roughly 5% of global foundry capacity — meaning every L4 vendor is racing against a shared, thin, and imperfect domestic wafer supply (confidence: C).

## Capability vs. global frontier (+ timeline)

- **Chip-for-chip:** Huawei's Ascend 910C — the most credible flagship — delivers roughly 60% of Nvidia H100 inference throughput per DeepSeek's own published benchmarking, with ~800 TFLOPS FP16 (~80% of H100 on paper spec) undermined by materially worse architectural efficiency (910C die logic area ~60% larger than H100 for comparable throughput) (C). Nvidia's own CEO has called the 910C "probably comparable to an H200" (C) — i.e., roughly one Nvidia generation behind at the single-chip level, with the gap persisting into 2026-2027 per Huawei's own roadmap (910C 2025 → 950PR 1Q26 → 950DT 4Q26 → 960 2027 → 970 2028) (D/C).
- **System-level, the gap narrows or inverts:** Huawei's CloudMatrix 384 supernode (384× Ascend 910C + 192 Kunpeng CPUs over a proprietary all-to-all optical "Unified Bus") delivers ~300 PFLOPS BF16 and 48TB pooled HBM — claimed to exceed Nvidia's GB200 NVL72 (~180 PFLOPS) (C). The next-gen Atlas 950 SuperPoD (8,192× Ascend 950DT) is claimed at 8 FP8 ExaFLOPS / 16 FP4 ExaFLOPS, a 20x jump over CloudMatrix 384 (C, vendor-disclosed, unverified independently). This "lose at the chip, win at the rack" strategy — brute-force interconnect and power to offset a ~1-1.5 generation silicon deficit — is Huawei's central bet and the most distinctive Chinese architectural choice versus the US frontier.
- **Software moat:** CUDA's 15+-year ecosystem lead remains the deepest bottleneck, deeper than the silicon gap. CANN (Huawei's compute architecture, paired with the MindSpore framework, plus a torch_npu PyTorch backend) has been substantially open-sourced during 2025-2026 to accelerate third-party adoption, but developer feedback still cites CANN's complexity, instability, and thin documentation/community relative to CUDA (C). The clearest capability proof point: Huawei's openPangu 2.0 (June 12, 2026) and Zhipu/Z.ai's GLM-5 are both reported as frontier-scale models trained entirely on Ascend + CANN/MindSpore with zero Nvidia dependency (C/D, vendor-adjacent reporting, not independently audited) — meaningful even if performance-per-dollar versus Nvidia-trained peers remains unverified. Cambricon's NeuWare stack separately claims full-stack compatibility with DeepSeek and Qwen models. **Timeline to software parity: realistically 3-5+ years** — the hardware gap is closing faster than the tooling gap.
- **Net read:** China is roughly 1-2 chip generations behind at the device level, plausibly at parity or ahead on rack-scale interconnect/power engineering for a subset of workloads, and still structurally behind on software portability and developer productivity. Market-cap gap remains enormous: Nvidia's own market cap (~$4-5tn range through 2026) dwarfs the entire domestic L4 cohort combined (low-to-mid tens of billions of dollars in aggregate, even after 2025-2026 IPO markups).

## Moats & unit economics

Huawei's moat is vertical integration plus state backing: it is the only domestic player with credible chip + interconnect + framework + cloud + model co-design, and it is fully insulated from IPO-market scrutiny because it is private. Cambricon's moat is first-mover software compatibility (DeepSeek/Qwen/GLM co-optimization) plus SMIC capacity priority, but unit economics remain unusual: FY2025 revenue of ~RMB6.5bn (~$900m) against a market cap that briefly exceeded RMB1 trillion (first STAR-listed company to do so, June 30, 2026) before correcting ~14% on a company risk warning — implying a valuation multiple detached from current fundamentals and highly sensitive to shipment/yield news (C). The merchant GPU cohort's moat is customer capture via the state procurement mandate rather than technical superiority: Kunlunxin's P800 reportedly took 70-100% share in specific government/SOE bid segments (China Mobile, Geely, China Southern Power Grid, China Merchants Bank), a function of policy-driven demand rather than open competition (C). Gross margins across the merchant cohort are not independently disclosed at sufficient granularity to benchmark against Nvidia's ~70%+ data-center gross margin; capex intensity (fabless design + priority SMIC wafer allocation + HBM sourcing workarounds) suggests materially thinner economics industry-wide.

## Bottlenecks & dependencies

1. **SMIC wafer capacity** is the shared chokepoint: current advanced-node (7nm-class) output is roughly 20-50k wafers/month depending on source, versus a stated national target of ~150-160k wpm by end-2027 (a "5x in two years" goal driven jointly by SMIC and Hua Hong) and 500k wpm by 2030 (C/D). Cambricon's own 2026 output plans have reportedly been throttled by SMIC allocation limits (C).
2. **HBM/advanced packaging** — indigenous HBM (Huawei's HiBL/HiZQ series) is immature relative to SK hynix/Samsung/Micron; this is Pod 2's primary subject but directly gates every L4 chip's usable throughput.
3. **CUDA-ecosystem lock-in** — even where domestic silicon is competitive on paper, migration cost for existing PyTorch/CUDA codebases remains the practical adoption barrier; open-sourcing CANN is a direct (partial) countermeasure.
4. **Nvidia policy whiplash** — the US allowed H200 sales to China in December 2025 at a 25% export tax, capped at ≤50% of total H200 units sold to US customers, with BIS case-by-case licensing (10 China firms cleared by May 2026); Blackwell/Rubin remain excluded (B). Meanwhile Beijing has moved in the opposite direction — an August 2025 50%-domestic-chip sourcing rule for data centers escalated to a full ban on foreign accelerators in state-funded projects by November 2025 (including retrofitting already-installed Nvidia/AMD/Intel hardware out of <30%-complete builds), and a draft ~$295bn national AI-computing-grid plan (up to ~$740bn including power infrastructure) targeting 80% domestic chips by 2028 (C). Reported Nvidia China data-center share fell from ~95% pre-controls to <60% in mid-2025 reporting and to what Nvidia's own CEO has since described as effectively zero in 2026 commentary — though ByteDance alone is separately reported still planning ~$14bn of 2026 Nvidia purchases alongside ~$5.6-5.7bn of Huawei Ascend purchases, suggesting the "zero" framing overstates a still-live (if shrinking and policy-fragile) legal channel (B/C, genuinely conflicting reports — flagged as an open uncertainty).
5. **Gray-market smuggling** — US DOJ charged Super Micro co-founder Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw and two others in March 2026 in a ~$2.5bn scheme routing restricted Nvidia-equipped servers to China; Taiwan raided Super Micro offices and a Japan-waypoint smuggling ring in May-June 2026; black-market DGX B300 prices in China reportedly doubled to >8m yuan (~$1.1m) over six months (B/C). This confirms real, unmet demand for top-tier Nvidia silicon that domestic supply cannot yet satisfy even amid the procurement mandate.
6. **Heterogeneous/disaggregated clusters** — prefill-decode disaggregation (compute-heavy prefill vs. bandwidth-heavy decode) is now standard LLM-serving architecture industry-wide (vLLM, SGLang, Nvidia Dynamo); Chinese operators are reportedly using it to blend domestic (decode/inference) and residual imported (prefill/training) accelerators within a single serving pipeline, though hard, source-confirmed China-specific deployment figures are not yet available (D, inferred from architecture trend + policy pressure, not directly confirmed for named Chinese deployments).

## Investability read

The domestic-GPU IPO wave (Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Iluvatar CoreX, and pending Kunlunxin) has been the standout L4 investment story of 2025-2026: every listing has popped 400-700%+ on debut, institutional books have been oversubscribed 20-30x and retail tranches 100s-2000x+, and combined implied market caps for four newly-listed names alone now approach $100bn+ against combined FY2025 revenues in the low hundreds of millions to low billions of dollars — a valuation structure that prices in years of state-mandated demand capture rather than current fundamentals (C). Cambricon's brief trillion-yuan cap and subsequent correction is the clearest evidence this segment trades on narrative/mandate momentum with high volatility. All merchant GPU names carry full or partial US Entity List exposure (Moore Threads and Biren explicitly named in the October 2023 Federal Register action; MetaX and Iluvatar face node/tooling restrictions even where not directly listed) — meaning US persons face restrictions on equity/debt financing support and these firms are permanently foreclosed from advanced US EDA/foundry access regardless of listing venue. Foreign-investor access varies sharply by listing venue: Hong Kong-listed names (Biren, Iluvatar) are directly accessible to foreign investors; STAR Market (Cambricon, Moore Threads, MetaX) requires Stock Connect/QFII access with narrower foreign float; Huawei and ByteDance remain fully closed to any foreign capital. Kunlunxin's unusual IPO structure — reportedly requiring investors to pre-commit chip purchases worth 3-7x their investment — is a notable governance flag for due diligence. Net: this is a policy-subsidized national-champion basket, not a pure-play bet on chip competitiveness; position sizing should discount headline pops heavily and price in Entity-List-driven capital-access and supply-chain fragility.
