# Pod 2 — Memory & Interconnect (L3: DRAM/NAND/HBM, L5: Interconnect & Networking)

**Analyst pod:** Pod 2 · **Date:** 2026-07-08 · **Data as-of:** mid-2026 unless noted
**Layers:** L3 (memory — DRAM, NAND, HBM) + L5 (interconnect — memory-interface chips, PCIe/CXL retimers, optical modules, scale-up/scale-out fabrics)

---

## 1. Scope

This memo covers China's two most capital-intensive, IPO-imminent hardware verticals: memory (CXMT for DRAM, YMTC for NAND, both mid-IPO-process on Shanghai's STAR Market in 2026) and interconnect (Montage Technology's global-leading memory-interface franchise, now dual-listed HK+A, plus Huawei's proprietary optical scale-up fabric for Ascend SuperPoDs and the domestic optical-module industry that increasingly supplies both Huawei and Nvidia supply chains). Both verticals sit directly on the AI capex supercycle: memory because HBM/DRAM is the single tightest global supply constraint on AI-chip output; interconnect because scale-up bandwidth, not FLOPs, increasingly gates cluster-level performance. The memo benchmarks CXMT/YMTC against Samsung/SK hynix/Micron and Montage/Huawei against Nvidia NVLink, Broadcom and Astera Labs, and closes with an investability read on CXMT (IPO-filed), YMTC (IPO-filed) and Montage (SSE:688008 + HKEX:6809).

## 2. Market structure & value chain

**Memory (L3).** China has exactly two scaled memory champions, both state-linked and both mid-IPO: **CXMT** (DRAM) and **YMTC** (3D NAND). CXMT cleared STAR Market IPO review on 27 May 2026, targeting a raise of ~RMB29.5bn (~$4.1bn) with post-listing valuation guidance clustering around **CNY2–3 trillion (~$280–420bn)** — a huge re-rating from the ~$42bn figure floated in January 2026, driven by a DRAM super-cycle: Q1-2026 revenue hit RMB50.8bn (~$7.1bn) with net profit of RMB24.76bn (~$3.5bn), +1,688% YoY, and fab utilization at 95.7% [WEB-001, C]. CXMT is now the **world's #4 DRAM maker with ~7.7% global share**, versus Samsung 40.5%, SK hynix 29.6% and Micron 19.9% [WEB-019, C]. It runs three 12-inch fabs on a G3 (~17.5nm-class) DDR5/LPDDR5X process at ~80% yield, is developing G4 (~16nm) and G5 nodes, and in 2026 debuted domestically-produced DDR5-8000 and LPDDR5X-10667 parts validated by MSI — the first credible signal that CXMT's mainstream DRAM is reaching commercial parity with Korean/US mainstream nodes (not yet at the leading edge) [WEB-002, C].

YMTC filed IPO tutoring paperwork with CITIC Securities on 19 May 2026 (Hubei CSRC office), with a formal STAR Market application possible by mid-2026; valuation estimates are unusually dispersed — from a conservative ~RMB300bn (~$44bn) "listing valuation" (Guosheng Securities) up to RMB500bn–800bn, and bull cases citing CNY2–3 trillion parity with CXMT [WEB-004, C — wide dispersion, treat point estimates as D]. YMTC is the world's **#3–4 NAND supplier at ~13–16% global share** [WEB-004, WEB-019, C]. Its strategic pivot: **Phase-3 capacity allocates ~50% to DRAM rather than NAND**, with TSV-based HBM packaging (hybrid bonding) under development via its Wuhan Xinxin Semiconductor Manufacturing (XMC) foundry, and LPDDR samples already out to customers [WEB-005, C]. Both companies are accelerating capacity: YMTC has advanced its Wuhan Phase-3 mass-production target from 2027 to end-2026 and plans two additional ~100k-wafer/month fabs; CXMT is building a new Shanghai fab (full mass production ~2027) that would roughly **double its combined DRAM+HBM capacity**; UBS estimates China's combined memory capacity could add 120–140k wafers/month in 2026 alone, with more in 2027 [WEB-006, C]. This underpins the seed database's "up to 10 memory fabs by 2030" estimate.

**HBM indigenization is the central strategic race**, and it depends on two prerequisites this pod's targets uniquely supply: a domestic DRAM process base (CXMT) and TSV/hybrid-bonding packaging capability (YMTC/XMC) — HBM is fundamentally stacked DRAM dies connected through-silicon, so a country without indigenous DRAM+TSV cannot indigenize HBM regardless of packaging-house capacity elsewhere. CXMT's roadmap: HBM2E as a 2026 bridge product (most domestic GPUs still ship with HBM2E, so this is commercially rational even though HBM2E is two generations behind the global frontier), HBM3 targeted for 2026 mass production but **slipping** — April-2026 reporting describes persistent low yields, delayed equipment deployment and a heat-dissipation defect limiting operating speed — with 12-layer (HBM3E-class) stacking now targeted for 2027 [WEB-003, C]. Realistic read: HBM2E-class volume in 2026, HBM3-class limited/low-yield production in 2026–27, HBM3E-class parity attempt in 2027–28 — roughly a 2–3 generation, 3–4 year lag to the SK hynix/Samsung/Micron frontier (which is already shipping HBM4 samples in 2026). Separately, Chinese researchers (IME CAS, Beijing Superstring Academy of Memory Technology, Shandong University) published a 4F² 2T0C DRAM-like cell architecture storing two bits/cell as a longer-horizon 3D-DRAM research path, but the report lacks manufacturing detail and should be read as a lab result, not a production roadmap [WEB-023, D].

**Interconnect (L5).** **Montage Technology** is the anchor name: the global #1 memory-interface chipmaker (RCD/MRCD/MDB/CKD chipsets that sit on every DDR5 server DIMM) with a **36.8% global market share** in 2025 [WEB-011, C], now expanding into PCIe/CXL retimers and, per its own roadmap, Ethernet PHY retimers — the "optical & Ethernet" expansion referenced in the brief. Montage completed a landmark **HKEX IPO in February 2026** (ticker 6809), raising HK$7.04bn (~$902m) — the largest Hong Kong IPO by a Chinese semiconductor company in ~20 years — priced at HK$106.89, a **44% discount** to the prior day's A-share close (not a premium) [WEB-007, C]. The HK stock then surged 64% on debut and has continued to re-rate: by late June/early July 2026 the HK line traded at HK$430.60 (mkt cap ~HK$374bn / ~$48bn) versus the A-share at CNY266.80 (mkt cap ~CNY328bn / ~$46bn) [WEB-008, C] — i.e., the two listings have converged to roughly parity on an FX-adjusted basis after starting at a steep IPO-price discount, which differs from the brief's framing of a persistent ~30% H-share premium; flag this as a point of drift between the brief's assumption and observed mid-2026 pricing.

Montage's core moat product lines (MRCD/MDB, PCIe Retimer, CKD, CXL MXC) now generate ~19% of interconnect revenue and grew 93.8% YoY in Q1-2026 [WEB-009, C]. Its Gen2 MRCD/MDB (12,800 MT/s, +45% vs Gen1) is in volume ramp, PCIe Retimer cumulative shipments exceed 1 million units (Montage is one of only two global suppliers of PCIe 5.0 retimers at scale), and its CXL MXC controllers are deployed by all three global memory majors in E3.S CXL modules [WEB-009, C]. In January 2026 it launched a PCIe 6.x/CXL 3.x retimer (M88RT61632, 64 GT/s) and a PCIe 6.x/CXL 3.x Active Electrical Cable (AEC) solution, and its stated roadmap extends to PCIe 7.0 retimers and **Ethernet PHY retimers** — the concrete evidence behind the brief's "expansion into optical & Ethernet" [WEB-010, C]. Montage sits on the JEDEC board, giving it standard-setting influence unusual for a Chinese semiconductor firm and reinforcing the switching-cost moat described below.

Separately, **Huawei** is building China's most credible answer to Nvidia's scale-up fabric at the system level, not the chip level: its Atlas 950 SuperPoD (available Q4 2026) links up to **8,192 Ascend 950DT NPUs** via its proprietary UnifiedBus/UB-Mesh protocol over an **all-optical interconnect**, spanning 160 cabinets (128 compute + 32 networking) across ~1,000m² and delivering 16 PB/s of aggregate interconnect bandwidth [WEB-012, C]. UB-Mesh is a recursive direct-connect topology (hybrid CLOS/2D-mesh) that Huawei claims scales sub-linearly in cost and achieves 95% efficiency at 8k-NPU scale versus Nvidia's current 3,584-GPU superpod ceiling; Huawei has stated an intent to open-source the UB-Mesh specification [WEB-013, C]. The already-shipping CloudMatrix 384 (910C-based) is the empirical precedent: 16 racks, all-internal-optical using 6,912 800Gbps LPO transceivers for >5.5 Pbps of internal bandwidth, delivering roughly 60% higher dense FP16 throughput and ~3.5x the HBM capacity of Nvidia's GB200 NVL72 — but at >4x the power draw and ~2.5x higher power per PFLOP [WEB-014, C]. This is the recurring China-compute pattern: competitive or superior raw system-level bandwidth/capacity, achieved by brute-force optical/power scaling rather than efficiency.

Below Montage and Huawei sits a fast-growing **domestic optical-module layer** that is arguably now more globally significant than any China-specific retimer/switch chip player: **Zhongji Innolight** and **Eoptolink** together supply roughly 60% of Nvidia's 800G optical-module orders and hold 7 of the global top-10 optical-transceiver vendor seats [WEB-015, C]. Q1-2026 revenue: Innolight RMB19.5bn (~$2.7bn, +192% YoY), Eoptolink RMB8.34bn (~$1.2bn, +106% YoY) [WEB-015, C]. 2026 is being called the "year of 1.6T" as the industry transitions from 800G to 1.6T pluggables, with co-packaged optics (CPO) as the next architectural shift both companies are investing into. Note these firms sell into *global* hyperscaler/Nvidia supply chains as much as into China's domestic stack — they are a China-domiciled chokepoint on the global AI buildout, not merely a domestic-substitution story, and should be read with that dual character. We did not find a scaled, named Chinese PCIe-retimer or Ethernet-switch-ASIC challenger to Montage/Astera Labs/Broadcom as of mid-2026 — this sub-segment (electrical retimers/switch silicon, as opposed to optical modules or memory-interface chips) remains a gap in China's L5 stack.

## 4. Moats & unit economics

- **Memory is a cyclical oligopoly, and CXMT/YMTC are entering it as price-takers turned marginal-supply-adders at the best possible moment.** The 2025-26 AI-driven DRAM/NAND super-cycle (Samsung/SK hynix/Micron each crossed $1 trillion market cap between May 6–27, 2026, combined ~$4.1 trillion, with 2026 YTD stock gains of 149%/215%/245% respectively [WEB-018, C]) means CXMT's 95.7% utilization and +1,688% YoY profit growth reflect industry-wide pricing power as much as company-specific execution — this is a favorable entry point for the IPOs but also means CXMT/YMTC earnings are exceptionally exposed to the next down-cycle, which memory has historically experienced every 2–4 years. Treat current hyper-growth financials as cycle-peak, not steady-state.
- **Montage has a genuine, durable, oligopolistic switching-cost moat** — once a memory-interface chipset (RCD/MRCD/MDB) is qualified into a DIMM/server platform by JEDEC-compliance testing and OEM validation, switching suppliers requires re-qualifying the entire memory subsystem, which is slow and risky for hyperscalers. Montage's JEDEC board seat lets it help write the specs its chips will implement — a rare position for a Chinese semiconductor firm and the reason its ~1/3 global share has proven durable against Rambus and Renesas for years. Its 62.2% gross margin (65.6% for interconnect specifically) and near-double-digit-billion RMB profit in 2025 [WEB-011, C] reflect that moat converting directly into unit economics, not just market share.
- **Huawei's optical/UB-Mesh strategy is a capital-and-power-substitution moat, not a technology-efficiency moat.** By using all-optical interconnect and brute-force cabinet/power scaling, Huawei can match or exceed Nvidia's bandwidth and memory-capacity metrics today, at the cost of 4x+ power draw — a trade that is economically viable in China (subsidized power, state-directed capex, sanctions-driven demand inelasticity) but would not be competitive in a normal commercial market. This is a policy-enabled moat, contingent on continued state support and Huawei's captive domestic customer base.
- **HBM indigenization economics are dominated by the DRAM+TSV prerequisite chain**: neither CXMT nor YMTC alone can indigenize HBM — CXMT needs YMTC/XMC-class TSV packaging, and TSV packaging needs CXMT-class (or comparable) domestic DRAM dies to stack. This cross-dependency is why Chinese HBM timelines keep slipping relative to single-company roadmaps: the bottleneck is systemic (packaging tooling, hybrid-bonding yield, thermal management) rather than resolvable by either company individually.
- **Optical-module makers (Innolight/Eoptolink) monetize a genuinely global, non-China-exclusive moat**: cost/manufacturing-scale leadership built serving Nvidia and global hyperscalers, which is now also feeding Huawei's domestic optical scale-up buildout. This is the one part of the L5 stack where China's advantage is not defensive/import-substitution but offensive/export-competitive.

## 5. Bottlenecks & dependencies

1. **HBM yield and thermal management remain the binding constraint on China's own AI-chip supply chain** — CXMT's HBM3 heat/yield issues directly cap how many Huawei Ascend 950-class chips can ship with domestic HBM, reinforcing chip-supply rationing already documented in Pod 1/Pod 3 [WEB-003, C].
2. **TSV/hybrid-bonding packaging capacity is a second, co-binding chokepoint** alongside DRAM process maturity — YMTC/XMC's TSV effort is still described as a "tech reserve" stage as of mid-2026, mirroring the advanced-packaging gap Pod 1 identifies at L1/L2.
3. **US Entity List and military-list exposure is fluid and adversarial for both memory champions.** CXMT is reportedly under active inter-agency consideration for Entity List addition (delayed for diplomatic reasons) and was named a "Chinese military company" under NDAA Section 1260H in June 2026, with a US government procurement ban effective 23 December 2027; the proposed MATCH Act would extend Entity-List-like export/service restrictions to all CXMT facilities [WEB-020, C]. YMTC has been on the Commerce Entity List since December 2022 and is litigating that designation in US federal court; its Pentagon 1260H status has flip-flopped within 2026 (apparently removed in February, reinstated in June) — treat as unresolved and monitor [WEB-021, C].
4. **Montage's Ethernet/optical expansion is early-stage** (engineering-sample tape-outs, not yet shipping) — the brief's framing of Montage as already competing in optical/Ethernet somewhat outruns the evidence; as of mid-2026 this is a stated roadmap item, not a revenue line.
5. **No scaled domestic PCIe-retimer/Ethernet-switch-ASIC pure-play beyond Montage was identified** — unlike memory-interface chips (where China leads) or optical modules (where China is competitive), the pure electrical-retimer/switch-silicon sub-segment of L5 remains structurally under-indigenized, leaving Astera Labs/Broadcom/Marvell as the effective global gatekeepers for any China buyer outside the Huawei ecosystem.
6. **Power efficiency, not bandwidth, is now Huawei's binding constraint** at the interconnect/system level — CloudMatrix 384's >4x power draw versus GB200 NVL72 means China's optical-scale-up advantage is capped by its lagging chip-process node and cannot be resolved purely through interconnect engineering.

## 7. Investability read

- **CXMT and YMTC are the two cleanest, most closely-watched pure-play China-memory IPO trades of 2026**, both STAR Market (Shanghai)-bound, both state-linked, both offering direct exposure to the domestic-DRAM/NAND-substitution and HBM-indigenization thesis. Foreign access will be restricted to Stock Connect/QFII channels once listed (consistent with other A-share national champions in this database). The CXMT valuation range (CNY2-3tn) implies one of the largest tech IPOs globally in 2026 if it lists near guidance — but the range itself has moved by >5x since January 2026 estimates, signaling genuine uncertainty rather than settled institutional consensus; treat pre-listing valuation figures as indicative, not tradeable.
- **Both companies carry acute and rising US policy risk that is structurally different from Pod 1's equipment names**: CXMT's near-miss Entity List addition and new DoD 1260H "military company" status, and YMTC's active Entity List litigation and flip-flopping Pentagon list status, mean geopolitical headline risk could reprice either IPO sharply and quickly, independent of operating performance. This is a bet on continued Chinese-market access and continued state support at least as much as a bet on the memory cycle itself.
- **Montage is the pod's highest-quality, already-investable name** — profitable, moat-protected, no Entity List taint, and (uniquely in this database) accessible to foreign investors directly via its new HK listing (6809.HK) as well as restricted A-share exposure. The Feb-2026 IPO's steep initial discount-to-A-share pricing (44%) followed by a 64% debut pop and subsequent convergence toward parity suggests the HK tranche was a genuine value opportunity at IPO, though most of that gap has likely already closed by the time of this writing (Jul-2026) — new entrants should not assume the same discount persists.
- **The domestic optical-module names (Innolight, Eoptolink) are a distinct, higher-beta way to play the same AI-buildout theme** with the added feature of substantial *non-China* revenue exposure (Nvidia/global hyperscaler orders) — arguably a better-diversified bet than pure domestic-substitution names, but also more exposed to a global AI-capex slowdown or to further US restrictions on optics/components sales to China-based suppliers of US customers.
- **The retimer/switch-ASIC sub-segment offers no clean China pure-play** — investors seeking that exposure remain limited to Astera Labs, Broadcom or Marvell (all non-China), underscoring that this specific L5 niche is a genuine, uninvested (from a China-thesis standpoint) gap as of mid-2026.
