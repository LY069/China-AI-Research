## 1. Scope

Pod 6 does not own a stack layer; it owns the rules of the game that every layer operates under. Three engines are mapped: (1) the Chinese state's industrial-policy machinery funding and mandating the AI build-out (the 15th Five-Year Plan, national funds, SOE/provincial mandates); (2) the US-led export-control regime and China's counter-leverage, which jointly set the *supply* constraint on frontier compute; and (3) the capital-markets channel — the 2025–2026 IPO wave that is now the primary vehicle turning China's AI-stack champions into investable (or semi-investable) securities. A short section (W) covers global diffusion of Chinese models/infrastructure as the soft-power complement to the domestic build-out. The deliverable is a single, report-wide investability/Entity-List table (§6) and a policy/funding/company data appendix.

## 2. Market structure & policy-capital architecture

**Policy stack (P).** China's AI industrial policy now runs on four tracks simultaneously: (a) the **15th Five-Year Plan** (drafted through 2025, ratified ~March 2026), which mentions AI roughly 4x more often than the 14th FYP and elevates AI diffusion — not frontier development — to the top strategic priority, targeting ~70% AI penetration of the economy by 2027 and ~90% by 2030, with the core AI industry targeted to grow from ~¥1.2tn (2025) to >¥10tn by 2030 [WEB-601, WEB-602]; (b) **mega-funds** — the ~$295bn (~¥2tn) nationwide AI data-center grid plan (NDRC-led, operated mainly by China Mobile/China Telecom, targeting ≥80% domestic technology content and network completion by 2028) [WEB-605, WEB-606], the ~$138bn (~¥1tn) National Venture Capital Guidance Fund for AI/robotics/hard-tech over 20 years (announced March 2025) [WEB-607, WEB-608], and the National IC "Big Fund," now in its third phase (Phase I ¥138.7bn/2014, Phase II ¥204bn/2019, Phase III ¥344bn/~$47.5bn/2024) [WEB-603, WEB-604]; (c) **mandates** — the ≥50% domestic-equipment-sourcing rule for new fab lines (already tracked) and an SOE AI-adoption push targeting ~70% AI penetration of key industrial sectors by 2027 [WEB-609]; and (d) the **"AI+" Initiative** (State Council Opinion, 26-Aug-2025, Doc. [2025] No. 11), which sets economy-wide smart-terminal/agent penetration targets of >70% by 2027, >90% by 2030, and a "fully intelligent economy" by 2035 [WEB-610]. Beneath the national layer, **provincial/municipal governments compete** via "computing-power vouchers" (Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Henan, Shandong, Chengdu, Ningbo) that subsidize up to 80% of AI compute-rental costs for SMEs — Shanghai alone allocated ~¥600m in compute vouchers plus ~¥100m for data/LLM training [WEB-611, WEB-612, WEB-613].

**Geopolitics (G).** The export-control regime has moved from a one-way ratchet (2022–2024) to a more contested, bidirectional one (2025–2026): Washington has both tightened (HBM, FDPR/SME, Entity List) and loosened (H20, H200) access, while Beijing has both weaponized and paused its own critical-minerals leverage. See the timeline in §4.

**Capital markets (K).** The 24-Sept-2024 policy pivot — a PBOC/CSRC/NFRA stimulus and governance package explicitly treating the equity market as a strategic asset — reset the multi-year bear narrative into what analysts call a "slow bull": managed, state-supported appreciation rather than a speculative boom [WEB-620, WEB-621]. That pivot created the conditions for a 2025–2026 **IPO wave** that has, for the first time, made a meaningful slice of the China AI stack — foundation-model labs and domestic GPU/accelerator designers — directly investable via public markets, chiefly Hong Kong and the Shanghai STAR Market. See the table in §5.

## 3. Capability vs. global frontier — the regulatory/capital dimension

China's *industrial-policy capacity* to mobilize capital toward AI arguably exceeds the US's in speed and scale (state funds alone total well over $500bn in headline commitments across the DC grid, AI+robotics fund, and Big Fund phases), but this is a volume advantage, not a capital-efficiency one — much of it is directed lending/guidance-fund capital with lower expected risk-adjusted returns than US venture/hyperscaler capex, which is overwhelmingly private and demand-driven. On the **regulatory/access dimension**, the US retains the decisive lever: export controls still bind China's frontier-compute supply (HBM, EUV-class litho, the fastest GPUs), even after the H20/H200 reversals, because those reversals apply to second-tier (not frontier) parts and remain revocable by policy. On the **capital-markets dimension**, China has closed ground quickly: the STAR Market's June-2026 "fifth listing standard" extension to loss-making large-model and quantum firms mirrors (with a national-security gate) the US's long-standing tolerance for pre-profit tech IPOs [WEB-631], and HKEX has absorbed a wave of GPU-designer and model-lab listings in under two quarters — a capital-formation speed the US-listed China-ADR channel cannot currently match given SEC/PCAOB and outbound-investment friction. **Timeline to parity:** on export-control durability, the binding constraint (EUV, HBM design/yield, CUDA-equivalent software) is unlikely to fully close before 2028–2030 per cross-pod estimates; on capital-markets access, China's IPO channel is arguably at rough parity with, or ahead of, the US's for indigenous-hardware listings *today*, but foreign-investor access to that channel is structurally capped by Entity-List overlap and the (still-developing) US outbound-investment security regime.

## 4. Geopolitics — export-control and counter-leverage timeline

- **Oct 2022:** First unprecedented BIS controls on advanced computing and semiconductor-manufacturing exports to China — the baseline restricting sub-14nm logic tools, HBM-adjacent memory tooling, and top-tier AI accelerators [WEB-614].
- **Oct 2023:** Controls tightened; loopholes for China-specific parts (e.g., A800/H800-class chips) closed via a "total processing performance/density" test; Entity List additions for AI/advanced-computing entities [WEB-614].
- **Dec 2024:** Major expansion — first-ever **HBM** country-wide controls (all HBM with memory-bandwidth density >2GB/mm², with a narrow US-headquartered-exporter exception up to 3.3GB/mm²); new **Foreign Direct Product Rule** layers (the "Footnote 5" FDP rule and the SME FDP rule) extending US jurisdiction to non-US-made semiconductor manufacturing equipment destined for China; ~140 additional entities, including SMIC affiliates, added under the new FN5 designation [WEB-615, WEB-616, WEB-619]. Huawei and other buyers reportedly pre-stockpiled ~7m Samsung HBM chips (>$1bn) ahead of the rule [WEB-615].
- **Jan 2025 (outgoing Biden admin):** An AI-diffusion tiered-country export framework issued; 11 entities added to the Entity List (10 for AI research, 1 for lithography), including **Zhipu AI** [WEB-617]. Separately, Treasury's **Outbound Investment Security Program (OISP)** final rule took effect 2-Jan-2025, covering US outbound investment into Chinese AI, semiconductor, and quantum entities [WEB-635, WEB-636].
- **Mar 2025:** Trump administration adds further entities (Federal Register 2025-05427, 12 entities) and signals a harder blacklisting posture on Chinese AI/chip firms [WEB-617, WEB-614].
- **Apr 2025:** Trump administration **prohibits H20 exports** to China (reversing the prior license-based allowance) [WEB-614].
- **Aug 2025:** H20 exports **reapproved**, conditioned on a 15% export-revenue fee to the US government [WEB-614].
- **Sep 2025:** BIS adds 32 entities to the Entity List (23 China-linked among the total), concentrated on military/dual-use and high-performance/AI-computing actors [WEB-618].
- **30-Oct-2025 (Busan):** Trump–Xi meeting yields a preliminary trade agreement that eases tariff and export-control escalation [WEB-614].
- **7-Nov-2025:** China **suspends** its rare-earth/gallium/germanium/antimony/superhard-material export controls on the US for one year (through ~10/27-Nov-2026) as part of the truce — a suspension, not a repeal, reactivable at will under Chinese administrative law [WEB-622, WEB-623].
- **Dec 2025:** NDAA FY2026 enacts the **Comprehensive Outbound Investment National Security Act**, broadening OISP's covered countries (adds Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, Venezuela) and covered sectors (adds HPC, supercomputing, hypersonics); implementing rules due within 450 days (~Mar 2027) [WEB-636, WEB-637]. On 8-Dec-2025, Trump announces **H200 exports to China will be permitted**.
- **15/16-Jan-2026:** BIS formal rule shifts H200 and AMD MI325X license review from presumption-of-denial to **case-by-case**, subject to a 25% tariff and security conditions (no reduction in US semiconductor capacity, buyer compliance program, third-party testing) [WEB-624, WEB-625].
- **1-Jun-2026:** Commerce clarifies export controls apply to **any company with a Chinese parent or HQ regardless of subsidiary location** — closing an overseas-subsidiary loophole [WEB-617].
- **Jun–Jul 2026:** China's own regulators tighten scrutiny of outbound tech/data deals and investigate AI startups (e.g., Manus) that relocated abroad for possible export-control violations; separately, Beijing is reported to be weighing **restrictions on foreign/overseas access to China's own leading AI models** — an emerging mirror-image control regime (single-source, developing story; confidence D) [WEB-638, WEB-639].

**Chip-specific:** Nvidia's China-specific **B30A** (Blackwell-derived, ~half of B300 throughput, 144GB HBM3E, single-die) remains in a gray zone — reportedly capable but its export approval is not yet confirmed as of mid-2026; likely priced $20–25k vs. H20's $10–12k if cleared [WEB-626, WEB-627]. **Smuggling/transshipment** remains material: US authorities have documented a ~$2.5bn Super Micro server-diversion network routed through Taiwan/Thailand/Hong Kong/mainland China using Southeast Asian shell companies, and separately estimate >$1bn of Nvidia B200-class chips reached China via gray-market channels since the April-2025 tightening [WEB-628, WEB-629]. **Taiwan flashpoint:** analyst consensus (ASPI, Recorded Future) treats a near-term (2025–2026) invasion as unlikely, with risk assessed to rise materially from 2027 onward; a blockade/quarantine short of invasion is considered the more probable coercive tool pre-2027. Modeled economic impact of a full conflict: ~$10.6tn global GDP hit (~9.6% of global GDP) in year one — do not read these as base-case probabilities, only as scenario-impact framing from third-party analysts [WEB-630, WEB-632, WEB-633].

**A/H premium & basket valuation.** The historical pattern — A-shares trading at a premium to H-shares for dual-listed names — is compressing broadly (the Hang Seng AH Premium Index is near 5-year lows) and has **inverted** for at least one AI-adjacent semi name (Montage, +14% H-premium by April 2026), reflecting a structural re-rating as global capital chases Hong Kong-listed China AI/semi exposure that mainland A-share limits (foreign-ownership caps, capital controls) restrict directly [WEB-653, WEB-654, WEB-659]. A composite "China AI infrastructure basket" — spanning equipment/EDA (NAURA, AMEC, Empyrean), foundry (SMIC, Hua Hong), memory (CXMT, YMTC once listed), AI chips (Cambricon, Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Iluvatar, Kunlunxin), interconnect (Montage), cloud (Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu), and models (Zhipu, MiniMax) — sits at a blended forward P/E in the high-teens on an HSTECH-index proxy basis (~19.9x vs. a 5-year average of ~22.5x) [WEB-660], but this blended multiple masks enormous internal dispersion: profitable incumbents (Alibaba, Tencent, Montage on an operating basis) trade at conventional double-digit multiples, while pre-revenue or barely-profitable GPU/model IPOs (MetaX, Moore Threads, Zhipu) carry effectively unmeasurable or triple-digit implied multiples driven by retail-momentum demand (MetaX's retail tranche and similar names have seen 500-2,300x oversubscription) [WEB-641, WEB-646].

**Foreign-investor access** runs through three channels, each constrained: (1) **Stock Connect** Northbound/Southbound links give global investors access to ~105-160 large, liquid dual-listed A-H names, but most of the new AI-stack IPOs are HK-only (not yet Stock-Connect-eligible) in their first 6-12 months; (2) **US persons** face the Treasury OISP (effective since Jan-2025, broadened by the Dec-2025 NDAA/COINS Act) which restricts or requires notification for US investment into Chinese AI/semiconductor/quantum entities — this increasingly overlaps with Entity-List-flagged names (Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Iluvatar, Cambricon, Kunlunxin are all entity_list=yes/partial per the company database), making a meaningful share of the "four little dragons" *functionally uninvestable* for US institutional capital even though their equity trades freely in Hong Kong for non-US holders; (3) China's own capital-account tightening is now bidirectional — Beijing is both curbing mainland retail access to *outbound* (US) equities (Jun-2026) and reportedly weighing limits on *foreign* access to its own top AI models (Jul-2026, single-source), while simultaneously the CSRC's Oct-2025 QFII two-year work plan aims to ease foreign institutional inbound access — a genuinely two-directional, still-unsettled policy stance [WEB-661, WEB-662, WEB-663, WEB-664].

## 7. Bottlenecks & dependencies

1. **Compute-supply chokepoint persists despite chip-rule reversals.** H20/H200 case-by-case approval is conditional and revocable; it does not restore frontier-tier (B200/B300-class) access, and the FDPR/SME/HBM controls from Dec-2024 remain the more durable constraint on China's ability to build its own next-generation accelerators.
2. **Entity-List/OISP overlap is shrinking the practically-investable universe** even as the IPO wave nominally expands the *listed* universe — most new GPU-designer IPOs are simultaneously Entity-List flagged, so the capital-markets story and the investability story are diverging for US-domiciled capital specifically.
3. **Valuation-versus-fundamentals gap** in the IPO wave (500–2,300x retail oversubscription, single-day gains of 400-700%, pre-profit issuers) raises correction risk that could spill into the broader "slow bull" narrative if a flagship listing (e.g., CXMT/YMTC, both multi-hundred-billion-dollar prospects) disappoints.
4. **Bidirectional capital-account tightening** (Beijing curbing outbound retail access to US equities; contemplating inbound curbs on foreign access to Chinese frontier models) is a genuine, still-unsettled swing factor for the "China AI basket as an accessible trade" thesis.
5. **Taiwan tail risk**, while assessed low-probability through 2026, is a discontinuous risk to the entire memory/foundry/GPU-listing complex given TSMC's centrality to every China AI-chip roadmap (Ascend, Cambricon fabrication exposure, packaging).
6. **Rare-earth leverage is currently "paused, not spent."** China's Nov-2025 suspension is time-boxed to Nov-2026 and reversible by administrative order, meaning it remains a live counter-escalation tool through the current truce window.

## 8. Investability read

The 2025–2026 IPO wave has structurally changed China AI-stack investability: a foundation-model lab (Zhipu) beat OpenAI to an IPO, and four domestic GPU designers went public in under seven months — a pace of capital formation for indigenous AI hardware that has no current US analog. But "listed" and "investable" have diverged for US-domiciled capital: OISP coverage plus Entity-List overlap effectively exclude US persons from the highest-momentum names (Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Kunlunxin), leaving non-US global capital (accessible via HKEX, increasingly via Stock Connect as names season) as the primary channel. Within the investable set, the more durable exposures are the profitable incumbents with real earnings (Alibaba, Tencent, Montage) trading at conventional multiples, versus the higher-beta, narrative-driven GPU/model IPO basket, where valuation dispersion is extreme and days-old public floats carry limited price discovery. The upcoming CXMT/YMTC STAR Market listings (potentially $40bn+ each) are the highest-visibility test of whether the wave's momentum survives contact with larger, more heavily scrutinized issuers.
