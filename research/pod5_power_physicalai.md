# Pod 5 — Power & Physical-AI (L0: Energy & Power, L9: Physical/Embodied AI)

**Analyst pod:** Pod 5 · **Date:** 2026-07-08 · **Data as-of:** mid-2026 unless noted
**Layers:** L0 (grid, generation, transmission, storage, DC power/cooling) + L9 (humanoid robotics, industrial/manufacturing AI, autonomous driving & robotaxi, embodied-AI foundation models)

---

## 1. Scope

This memo covers the two layers of China's AI stack where the country's structural endowments — rather than its semiconductor deficits — are most plausibly decisive. **L0 (power)** asks whether China's electricity abundance can offset, or even outrun, its compute-chip constraints: it covers grid scale, generation mix, curtailed-renewable DC siting (East-Data-West-Compute), UHV transmission, grid storage (CATL), and the emerging "power ceiling" debate in which the US may hold a compute-architecture lead it cannot fully power. **L9 (physical AI)** covers humanoid robotics (Unitree, UBTech, Fourier Intelligence, AgiBot/Zhiyuan, Galbot), industrial/manufacturing AI, autonomous driving and robotaxi (Baidu Apollo Go, Pony.ai, WeRide, Momenta), and the policy logic — the ~$138bn 20-year AI+robotics fund and ~¥1tn robotics/high-tech program — behind China's decision to frame AI primarily as physical-world infrastructure for manufacturing rather than as a services/superintelligence race. Six investable names anchor the section: UBTech (HKEX:9880), Unitree (STAR Market IPO, pending), Horizon Robotics (HKEX:9660), Pony.ai/WeRide (US ADRs), CATL (SZSE:300750/HKEX:3750), and State Grid (unlisted, SOE).

## 2. Market structure & value chain

**Power/grid.** China generated **>10,000 TWh** of electricity in 2024 versus the US's **~4,600 TWh** — more than double, and more than the US, EU and India combined [WEB-001, C]. In 2025 China added a further ~500 TWh of generation, almost all from wind and solar [WEB-001, C]. The generation mix as of 2025 is **coal ~55%** (down from ~59% in 2024), **hydro ~14%**, **solar ~12%**, **wind ~10%**, **nuclear ~4.5%** [WEB-020, C]; in H1 2025, total renewable generation (5,072 TWh) surpassed coal generation (4,896 TWh) for the first time [WEB-024, C]. Installed wind+solar capacity hit **1,840 GW** at end-2025 (311 GW solar + 119 GW wind added that year, +22% YoY), now **47.3%** of total installed capacity and having overtaken thermal capacity for the first time [WEB-024, C]. **State Grid Corp** (unlisted SOE) is committing **CNY 4tn (~$580bn)** to grid capex over the 15th FYP (2026-2030), a 40% step-up, funding 15 new UHV lines that will add ~200 GWh/year of renewable-power connection capacity and lift cross-provincial transmission capacity 30-35% [WEB-005, C]. **East-Data-West-Compute (东数西算)**, the flagship program siting data centers on curtailed/cheap western renewable power, has reached **63% occupancy** across its eight hub nodes with inter-hub latency down to 20ms and cluster compute exceeding 50,000 petaflops at some western hubs (Qingyang, Zhongwei); cumulative investment is ~CNY239bn (~$33bn) [WEB-003, C]. The economic logic is curtailment: solar/wind curtailment rose to **9.2%/8.5%** respectively in Jan-Feb 2026 (from 6.1%/6.2% a year earlier), against a national 10% curtailment ceiling (relaxed from 5% in 2024) [WEB-004, C] — i.e., China has a growing, policy-tolerated pool of "free" renewable electrons that data-center siting is being directed toward. **CATL** (SZSE:300750/HKEX:3750), combined market cap **>RMB3tn (~$420bn)** as of April 2026, is the grid-storage linchpin: energy-storage-system (ESS) revenue reached **RMB62.4bn (~$8.6bn, 14.7% of total revenue)** in 2025 on 121 GWh shipped (+29% YoY, #1 globally for a 5th straight year), and management guides ESS to reach ~50% of global sales by 2030 (from ~25% today) [WEB-006, C].

**Physical AI/L9.** China's robotics base is already the world's largest by volume: it installed **295,000 industrial robots in 2024 (54% of the global total)**, with an operating stock >2m units and robot density (470/10,000 manufacturing workers) now above Germany and Japan [prior search context, C]. On humanoids, five companies dominate deal flow and shipment share: **Unitree** (STAR Market IPO approved July 2026 in a record 104 days, targeting ~RMB40-42bn/~$5.9-6.2bn valuation on 2025 revenue of RMB1.69bn, +335% YoY — the world's top-shipping humanoid maker) [WEB-008, C]; **UBTech** (HKEX:9880, 2026 revenue forecast raised to CNY3.69bn, +89%, on its mass-production-oriented UWORLD U1 series which has logged >13,000 cumulative unit orders) [WEB-009, C]; **AgiBot/Zhiyuan Robotics** (private, ~$6.4bn valuation as of July 2026, backed by Tencent/BYD/Hillhouse, planning an HK IPO in H2 2026 to raise >$1bn) [WEB-011, C]; **Galbot** ($3bn valuation after a $300m+ Dec-2025 round, $800m raised cumulatively, deployed wheeled humanoids at CATL/Bosch/Toyota) [WEB-012, C]; and **Fourier Intelligence** (~$1.1bn pre-money valuation, SoftBank Vision Fund-backed) [WEB-012, C]. On autonomous driving/robotaxi — the layer most tightly linked to China's EV manufacturing base — **Baidu Apollo Go** leads by scale (peak >350,000 weekly rides, >22m cumulative rides, live in 27 cities plus South Korea, pending UK/Switzerland pilots) [WEB-013, C]; **Pony.ai** (Nasdaq/HKEX dual-listed) posted Q1 2026 revenue +145% YoY to $34.3m and raised its 2026 robotaxi fleet target to 3,500 vehicles [WEB-014, C]; **WeRide** (Nasdaq:WRD, market cap ~$2.44bn) posted Q1 2026 revenue +58% to $16.5m with a ~1,300-vehicle global fleet targeting 2,600 by year-end [WEB-015, C]; and **Momenta** (newly listed HKEX:6880, July 8 2026 debut) raised $751m at a ~$9bn valuation, backed by GM/Tencent/Mercedes-Benz/BlackRock/GIC, and holds ~64.5% global independent-supplier share in urban NOA (navigate-on-autopilot) software running in >733,000 vehicles [WEB-016, C].

## 4. Moats & unit economics

- **The power thesis is an arbitrage, not just an abundance story.** China's moat is not simply "more electricity" but electricity that is *curtailed and therefore near-zero marginal cost* in specific western provinces, combined with UHV infrastructure and East-Data-West-Compute siting policy that can route AI-DC demand to exactly those electrons. This converts a renewables-integration problem (curtailment) into an AI-infrastructure subsidy — a distinctive policy-engineered unit-economics advantage with no clean US analog, since US DC siting is driven by fiber/tax/latency logic rather than curtailed-power arbitrage.
- **CATL's storage moat compounds the power thesis**: as more intermittent solar/wind comes online, grid-scale storage becomes the binding constraint on how much curtailed power can be time-shifted into firm DC supply; CATL's scale (121 GWh, 5x-running #1 global share) gives it first claim on the input goods (cells, BMS) needed to convert curtailment into dispatchable DC power, and its ESS margins benefit from the same manufacturing scale that underpins its EV-battery dominance.
- **State Grid is an unlisted, un-investable monopoly moat.** Its $580bn 15FYP capex program is the physical precondition for both the power thesis and the China EV/robotics manufacturing base, but as a 100%-state entity it captures none of the equity upside for outside investors — the investable proxies are downstream (CATL) or adjacent (UHV-equipment suppliers not covered in this pod).
- **Humanoid robotics unit economics remain pre-profit across the board.** UBTech is guided to first-ever profitability only by 2028; Horizon Robotics posted a **RMB10.47bn loss in 2025** against RMB3.76bn revenue (R&D alone was 137% of revenue) [WEB-010, C]; AgiBot, Galbot and Fourier are all pre-IPO cash-burning private companies. The moat, to the extent one exists, is manufacturing-cost and BOM advantage inherited from China's EV/battery/motor supply chain (the same logic Tesla claims for Optimus in the US) — Unitree and UBTech ship humanoids at price points (sub-$50k, some sub-$20k for smaller form factors) well below Figure/1X/Apptronik's implied unit costs, which is a genuine China-specific cost advantage rather than a pure subsidy artifact.
- **Robotaxi unit economics are inflecting but still small.** Pony.ai and WeRide are both posting triple-digit revenue growth off tiny bases ($16-34m/quarter) with fleets in the 1,300-3,500 range; Momenta's newly-IPO'd software-licensing model (64.5% global independent-supplier NOA share, 733k vehicles) is closer to a scalable-margin SaaS-like structure than the hardware-heavy robotaxi operators, and is the most capital-efficient path to profitability in the group. Baidu's Apollo Go has the clearest scale lead (>22m rides) but Baidu does not break out Apollo Go unit economics separately, so profitability at the ride level is unverified (D-grade inference).
- **Policy is the explicit moat-builder at L9**: the ~$138bn 20-year AI+robotics state venture fund (announced 2025, NDRC-run "state venture capital guidance fund") and the ~¥1tn robotics/high-tech investment program are designed to seed exactly the AgiBot/Galbot/Unitree tier of company, while new MIIT national standards (a dedicated Humanoid Robot and Embodied Intelligence Standardization Technical Committee, first standards released March 2026) aim to make Chinese humanoid specs the basis for global interoperability standards — a moat-by-standardization strategy analogous to China's earlier EV/battery playbook [WEB-017, WEB-018, C].

## 5. Bottlenecks & dependencies

1. **The power advantage is necessary but not sufficient — it only pays off if the domestic-chip gap closes.** Abundant, cheap, curtailed power is irrelevant to AI capability if Ascend/Cambricon-class chips remain supply-constrained (per Pod 3/1, domestic AI chip production ~2m units/2026 vs demand >5m). The "does latent power let China's DC buildout outrun the US" thesis is conditional, not unconditional [inference from Pod 1/3 seed data + WEB-007, D].
2. **Grid-to-DC "last mile" delivery, not generation, is now the tighter constraint in specific corridors.** Even with abundant western generation, UHV transmission capacity, substation buildout, and cooling-water availability at inland sites can lag demand — the $580bn 15FYP grid capex is explicitly targeted at this gap, implying it is not yet closed [WEB-005, C].
3. **Curtailment-based DC siting concentrates AI infrastructure geographically and creates a latency/talent trade-off** (western provinces vs. coastal population/talent centers) that East-Data-West-Compute's 20ms inter-hub latency figure only partially resolves.
4. **Humanoid-robotics profitability is 2+ years out for every major private player**, and the sector's ~$3.7bn of 2025 global funding (+169% YoY) is running well ahead of revenue — a valuation/burn-rate risk shared with US peers (Figure, 1X) but more acute for pre-IPO Chinese names without listed-market discipline.
5. **Autonomous-driving regulatory reciprocity is a real constraint on the global-expansion thesis**: Apollo Go/Pony.ai/WeRide international expansion (South Korea, UK, Switzerland, UAE, Singapore) depends on foreign regulators granting L4 permits to Chinese-designed stacks — a process that is going well so far in 2026 but is reversible on any China-tech-security backlash.
6. **Entity-List/export-control spillover risk into L9 is emerging but not yet acute**: none of the six anchor L9 companies carry a confirmed Entity-List flag as of this writing, but the same "China AI/robotics as strategic industry" framing that is drawing US Congressional attention to chips (MATCH Act, per Pod 1) could plausibly extend to humanoid-robotics or AV-software restrictions given the sector's designation as a national-fund priority.

## 7. Investability read

- **CATL is the cleanest, largest, most liquid pure-play on the power thesis** — dual-listed (A+H), foreign-investable, ~$420bn market cap, with a clear multi-year ESS growth runway (25%→50% of sales by 2030) tied directly to the curtailment-arbitrage/DC-power story. It is also the pod's least controversial Entity-List exposure (none identified).
- **State Grid is the power-thesis linchpin but is categorically uninvestable** (100% state-owned, no listed vehicle) — exposure to the UHV/grid buildout theme for outside investors has to be taken indirectly, via equipment suppliers not covered in this pod (transformer/HVDC-converter makers) or via CATL.
- **Momenta, freshly listed (HKEX:6880, debuted 8-Jul-2026, +1.8% to +3% on debut), is the most capital-efficient L9 name** on offer: a licensed-software model with 64.5% independent-supplier NOA share and blue-chip cornerstone investors (GIC, Fidelity, BlackRock, Mercedes-Benz, BYD) gives it the closest thing in this pod to a scalable-margin structure, at a ~$9bn valuation that already prices in significant growth.
- **Unitree's imminent STAR Market IPO is the marquee humanoid-robotics listing to watch** — approved in a record 104 days, it will be the first large, liquid, A-share-listed pure-play on China's humanoid volume leadership; expect restricted foreign access (A-share) similar to other STAR names in this program.
- **UBTech (HKEX:9880) is the most foreign-accessible listed humanoid pure play today**, though it remains pre-profit (breakeven not guided until 2028) — best read as a growth/optionality position rather than a current-earnings story.
- **Pony.ai and WeRide (both US-listed ADRs)** offer the most direct, liquid US-investor access to China's robotaxi scale-up, but both remain small-revenue, loss-making, and exposed to US-listing/ADR delisting-risk headlines that are idiosyncratic to Chinese ADRs generally (not covered in depth here; see Pod 6 for capital-markets/ADR risk).
- **AgiBot and Galbot are pre-IPO/private** — no public equity access yet; AgiBot's planned HK IPO (H2 2026, targeting >$1bn raise) is the name to track for a first liquid entry point into the highest-momentum private humanoid name.
- **Cross-cutting theme:** the power layer (CATL, State Grid) is a "picks and shovels" bet on China's AI buildout *regardless* of which application layer wins, while the L9 layer is a levered, still-unprofitable bet on China's physical-AI thesis specifically — investors seeking the power-abundance thesis with lower volatility should prefer CATL/grid-equipment exposure over the humanoid-robotics basket.
