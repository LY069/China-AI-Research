# P6 — Index & Reference Data: Where China's AI Stack Sits in the Benchmarks

## Scope

This is a structured-data pod, not a capability or investability narrative pod (those are P1–P5). The task: for every **listed** company in `database/company_master.csv` (29 of 44 rows — the rest are private, IPO-filed, IPO-approved-but-not-trading, or unlisted SOEs), compile ISIN, GICS sector/industry, membership in five benchmark indices (MSCI China, MSCI China A Onshore, CSI 300, Hang Seng Index (HSI), Hang Seng TECH (HSTECH)), approximate index weight where knowable, and market cap as a share of the relevant listing market — then derive the headline insight the brief asks for: **how concentrated is China's investable AI stack inside the flagship benchmarks a global allocator actually holds?**

The short answer: **very**. A handful of AI-stack names — mostly the platform giants (Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu) plus a small cluster of chip/EDA/optical names that have re-rated sharply in 2025–2026 — now account for a striking share of CSI 300, HSTECH, and MSCI China weight. That concentration is itself a risk factor worth flagging to allocators, independent of the underlying AI-capability story covered in P1–P5.

**Methodological note up front:** every attempt to fetch a primary index-provider factsheet directly (msci.com PDFs, hsi.com.hk PDFs, csindex.com.cn PDFs, Wikipedia's HSI/CSI300 pages, stockanalysis.com's ETF holdings pages) returned **HTTP 403** to automated retrieval — these providers block non-browser fetches. All index-membership and weight data below was instead obtained via web search, which surfaces search-engine summaries of those same factsheets (dated, named, and independently corroborated across multiple hits in most cases) plus financial-aggregator sites (cbonds, MarketScreener, stockanalysis, Yahoo Finance, Eulerpool, DivvyDiary) for ISINs. Per the v2 sourcing rule, **none of this qualifies as A/B** (no direct primary-document access) — everything here is graded **C at best, D where estimated or unconfirmed**. Where I could not locate a figure at all, I say so explicitly rather than estimate a plausible-looking number, per the "do not synthesize precise figures" mandate. Index constituents and weights also move continuously (semi-annual/quarterly reviews plus organic price action); every weight cited carries an as-of date and should be treated as a point-in-time snapshot, not a current live figure.

## Index-by-index findings

### CSI 300 (onshore A-share blue-chip benchmark)
The single most striking, best-corroborated data point in this exercise: **Zhongji Innolight (optical transceivers) became CSI 300's single largest-weighted constituent in early June 2026, at roughly 5.0%, overtaking CATL (4.1%)** (WEB-936). That a 300-name index tracking the entire onshore A-share market — historically dominated by banks, insurers, and liquor — now has an 800G/1.6T optical-module maker as its top holding is a direct, mechanical readout of how much of 2025–2026's AI-driven re-rating has flowed into CSI 300 float-adjusted weight. Add **Cambricon and Empyrean**, both added to CSI 300 in the December 2023 semi-annual review alongside Hygon (WEB-935) — Cambricon's market cap alone has since grown to ~$120bn (per `company_master.csv`, briefly crossing RMB1tn before a ~14% correction) — and CSI 300's identifiable AI-stack exposure (Innolight + CATL + Cambricon + Empyrean, the four names with confirmed membership) is **conservatively in the high single digits to low double digits of total index weight**, even before accounting for NAURA, AMEC, Montage, or SMIC's A-share line, none of which could be confirmed as CSI 300 members this cycle (plausible given their size, but unconfirmed — treat as upside to this estimate, not included in the sum).

### MSCI China (the standard international benchmark)
Per the MSCI China Index (USD) factsheet dated ~29-May-2026 (WEB-931): **Tencent 13.35% and Alibaba 10.17% are the #1 and #2 constituents, together 23.52% of the entire index.** Both are core AI-stack names in this database (Hunyuan/Tencent Cloud; T-Head chips/Alibaba Cloud/Qwen) — but both are also diversified conglomerates where AI/cloud is one segment among several (gaming/fintech/advertising for Tencent; e-commerce/logistics for Alibaba — see P1/P2 for the AI-specific revenue split). Baidu and SMIC are very likely additional MSCI China constituents at their scale, but no numeric weight could be located this cycle. **A-shares are only partially represented in the standard MSCI China Index** via a phased inclusion factor (raised to 20% in three tranches through November 2019); this means NAURA, Cambricon, Innolight etc. have *some* presence in MSCI China's headline number, but at a heavily dampened weight relative to their onshore CSI 300 / MSCI China A Onshore weights — I was not able to locate a confirmed post-2019 MSCI China (not "A Onshore") weight for any pure-play A-share chip name, so I have marked these "yes (partial, inferred)" rather than asserting a number.

### MSCI China A Onshore (the onshore-only counterpart)
This is where the pure-play chip/EDA/optical names actually show up with confirmed, sourced weights: **Cambricon 1.60%** (MSCI China A Index, USD, ~Apr-2026) and **0.75%** (broader A Onshore IMI); **Zhongji Innolight 1.51%** (A Onshore IMI); **NAURA 1.67%/0.72%** (per the "China A Inclusion Top 200 Select Governance" factsheet, Sep-2025, note this is a slightly different named index than the headline A Onshore benchmark — flagged in the data table). Summing just these three confirmed pure-play names gives **roughly 3.9–4.9% of a benchmark that historically skewed toward financials, industrials, and consumer staples** — a real, sourced concentration shift, even before NAURA/AMEC/Montage/CATL's (likely larger, unconfirmed) A-share weights are added.

### Hang Seng Index (HSI) — Hong Kong blue-chip benchmark
The 22-May-2026 review (effective 8-Jun-2026) expanded HSI from 90 to 93 constituents and explicitly named **Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu, Xiaomi, and SMIC as subject to an "8% single-name weight recap"** (WEB-934) — meaning each is large enough to bump against HSI's concentration cap, a strong (if imprecise) signal that AI-stack names collectively occupy a very large share of the index, plausibly in the 20–30%+ range if Alibaba, Tencent, and Baidu are each near or at the 8% ceiling. The one number I could pin down precisely: **CATL's H-share (3750.HK) carries a 0.81% HSI weight** post-rebalance (Free Availability Factor 95%, ~20-May-2026, WEB-934) — small on its own, illustrating that even a $420bn-market-cap battery/grid-storage champion is a rounding error next to the platform giants in a cap-weighted, capped index.

### Hang Seng TECH (HSTECH) — the 30 largest HK-listed tech names
The most AI-stack-concentrated of the five indices by construction (it's a tech-only index). Confirmed weights from the ~May-2026 factsheet (WEB-933): **SMIC 9.54%** (the single largest constituent), **Tencent 8.35%**. Two of the other top-5 names cited in the same factsheet snapshot — NetEase (9.27%) and Meituan (7.34%) — are **not** AI-stack names under this database's definition (gaming and food-delivery respectively) and are excluded from any AI-stack sum despite their large HSTECH weights; this is a useful caution against conflating "HK tech index" with "AI stack." Alibaba is very likely also a top-5-to-top-8 HSTECH weight given its scale, but no specific 2026 figure was located. Taken together, **SMIC + Tencent alone are ~17.9% of HSTECH**, and the true AI-stack total (adding an unconfirmed but plausible Alibaba weight) is very likely closer to **a quarter to a third of the entire index** — meaning an investor buying a generic "China tech" HSTECH tracker is already making a large, concentrated AI-stack bet, whether or not that's the intent.

## Investability read

Index membership is a reasonable proxy for **passive-flow investability**: a name inside MSCI China, CSI 300, HSI, or HSTECH gets systematic buying from trillions of dollars in index-tracking AUM, regardless of active-manager conviction. On that lens: **the platform giants (Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu) and SMIC are fully "in" every relevant benchmark** and see the largest passive flows. **CATL and Innolight are "in" via CSI 300 and (CATL) HSI**, giving them onshore- and HK-side passive support. **Cambricon, Empyrean, NAURA sit inside CSI 300 and/or MSCI China A Onshore** — meaningful onshore passive exposure, but largely absent from the MSCI China headline index that most global (ex-China-dedicated) allocators actually benchmark against, given the A-share partial-inclusion factor. **The entire recent-IPO cohort — Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Iluvatar, Zhipu, MiniMax, Momenta, and (once listed) CXMT/YMTC/Unitree — sits outside every index examined here**, meaning their extraordinary 2025–2026 share-price moves (some >400–700% since listing, per `company_master.csv`) have been driven almost entirely by active/speculative flow, not passive index buying. That is a materially different risk profile from the index-anchored names, and a fact a global-macro reader should weigh heavily: these names could see a *second* re-rating leg if/when they clear the market-cap and liquidity bars for MSCI or CSI inclusion at the next scheduled review — or could see outsized volatility on the way there with no passive floor underneath them.

## GLOSSARY

- **GICS (Global Industry Classification Standard):** the sector/industry taxonomy jointly maintained by MSCI and S&P, used to classify every listed company into a Sector → Industry Group → Industry → Sub-Industry hierarchy; the basis for this pod's `gics_sector`/`gics_industry` columns.
- **ISIN (International Securities Identification Number):** a 12-character alphanumeric code uniquely identifying a specific security (e.g., one share class of one company on one listing venue) — the standard cross-border security identifier; a single company can have multiple ISINs if it has multiple listing lines (e.g., A-share + H-share + ADR).
- **MSCI China vs. MSCI China A:** MSCI China is the standard international benchmark covering H-shares, Red-chips, P-chips, and ADRs, plus a *partial* allocation of onshore A-shares (via the "A-share inclusion factor," raised to 20% through Nov-2019); MSCI China A Onshore is a separate, A-share-only index covering the full onshore-listed universe at (near-)full weight — the two indices track substantially different, only partially overlapping, sets of effective exposure.
- **Free float:** the portion of a company's outstanding shares that are freely tradable on the open market (excluding strategic/founder/government/lock-up holdings); most modern indices (MSCI, HSI, HSTECH, CSI 300) weight constituents by *free-float-adjusted* market cap, not total market cap, so a company with a large headline market cap but low free float (e.g., a state-controlled SOE or a recently-IPO'd company still under lock-up) can carry a disproportionately small index weight.
- **CSI 300:** China Securities Index Company's flagship onshore benchmark, tracking the 300 largest and most liquid A-shares across the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges combined; reviewed semi-annually (June/December).
- **HSI / HSTECH:** the Hang Seng Index (Hong Kong's blue-chip benchmark, ~90-93 constituents) and Hang Seng TECH Index (the 30 largest HK-listed technology names, a sub-index), both maintained by Hang Seng Indexes Company; both apply single-name weight caps ("recapping") to limit concentration.
- **Stock Connect / A-share vs. H-share:** an A-share is a mainland-China-listed, RMB-denominated share (Shanghai or Shenzhen exchange); an H-share is the same underlying company's Hong Kong-listed, HKD-denominated share class — many large Chinese companies (CATL, Montage, SMIC) maintain both, often at different valuations (see P5 for the investability/access-mechanics deep dive).

## LIMITATIONS

- **No primary-source documents were successfully fetched.** Every attempt to retrieve an MSCI, Hang Seng Indexes, or CSI factsheet PDF directly returned HTTP 403 (these sites block automated/non-browser fetches); Wikipedia's HSI and CSI 300 pages and a major ETF-holdings page (stockanalysis.com) also returned 403. All index data in this pod is therefore search-engine-mediated — I have cited the underlying primary document by name and date wherever the search summary specified one, but I could not independently verify the search engine's summary against the source document itself. This is the single biggest caveat on this entire deliverable and the reason every index-weight figure here is graded C, never A/B.
- **ISINs are sourced entirely from financial-data aggregators** (cbonds, MarketScreener, stockanalysis, Yahoo Finance, Eulerpool, DivvyDiary), not from exchanges, registrars, or ANNA/national numbering agencies directly. These aggregators generally agree with each other where cross-checked, which increases confidence, but none constitutes a primary/authoritative source under the v2 confidence rubric — hence C, not A/B, throughout.
- **Most small/mid-cap and all newly-listed (Dec-2025–Jul-2026) names have no confirmed index membership data**, not because they are necessarily excluded, but because I could not locate a sourced answer either way within a reasonable search budget. Where the table says "not confirmed this cycle," treat that as a genuine data gap, not a confirmed "no" — except where a company's listing date makes "no" a near-certainty (e.g., Momenta, listed 8-Jul-2026, two days before this table's compilation).
- **`mktcap_pct_market` denominators (HKEX/SSE/SZSE aggregate market caps) are themselves poorly sourced and highly dispersed** across the search results obtained (HKEX ranged from HK$43.3tn to HK$49.9tn across different 2026 snapshots; the SSE figure of ~$10.2tn required discarding an initial ~$26.6tn "total market capitalization" figure that likely included bonds/funds, not just equities). Every `mktcap_pct_market` figure in the data file should be read as an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a precise ratio.
- **GICS sub-industry assignments for newly-listed/hybrid business models** (Zhipu, MiniMax, Pony.ai, WeRide, Momenta, Horizon Robotics, UBTech) are this analyst's best-fit judgment calls based on business description, not confirmed against an actual GICS classification lookup — flagged D throughout and should not be relied on for anything requiring an authoritative GICS code (e.g., a compliance/mandate screen).
- **The "AI-stack exposure sums" in this memo (e.g., "~9% of CSI 300," "23.52% of MSCI China," "17.9%+ of HSTECH") are additive combinations of individually-sourced weights, not a single sourced aggregate figure** — no index provider publishes an "AI-stack" sector bucket, so these sums necessarily reflect this database's own stack-layer taxonomy (`company_master.csv` `layers` field) rather than any official classification. A different definition of "AI stack" (narrower — chips only; or broader — including EV/robotics adjacent names) would produce a different sum.
- **Currency/date mismatches:** company market caps in `company_master.csv` carry their own individual as-of dates (ranging from Mar-2025 to Jul-2026) and are not restated to a common date; index weights likewise reflect different factsheet vintages (Sep-2025 through Jun-2026). Cross-referencing a Sep-2025 index weight against a Jul-2026 market cap (as happens in a few rows) is a known inconsistency, flagged inline where material.
