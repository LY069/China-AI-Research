## Scope

v1 mapped *who makes what* in China's AI stack. This pod answers the question v1 left open: **who actually pays for it, and is the growth the market has priced in consistent with that answer?** Two deliverables, per the brief: (1) for each major listed name across Pods 1–3, an estimated split between **domestic-substitution demand** (Chinese fabs/clouds/state buyers substituting for now-restricted foreign suppliers) and **global/external AI demand** (revenue earned by selling into the worldwide, largely Nvidia-centric AI buildout); (2) for the ~10 most investable names, **actual vs. guidance vs. sell-side consensus** revenue/earnings growth, benchmarked against the closest global peer's own actual/guidance/consensus, to show what growth trajectory each market is pricing in.

**Central finding:** the "China AI stack" is not one trade. It splits cleanly into (a) **domestic-substitution plays** (NAURA, AMEC, ACM, SMIC, Cambricon, the merchant-GPU IPO cohort, Kunlunxin) whose revenue is a bet on Beijing's localization mandates holding, insulated from (but also unable to benefit from) the global AI capex cycle directly; and (b) **global-AI-supply-chain plays domiciled in China** (Montage, Zhongji Innolight, Eoptolink) that are genuinely riding the same Nvidia/hyperscaler capex wave as Astera Labs or Coherent, with revenue majority-sourced overseas. Treating these as one basket materially misprices the geopolitical-fragility profile of each.

### Reading the split

Three tiers emerge, and they carry three different risk profiles:

1. **Pure domestic-substitution (Cambricon, Moore Threads/MetaX/Biren/Iluvatar, Kunlunxin, ACM Research, ~SMIC):** revenue is a direct function of (a) the state procurement mandate holding and (b) continued Entity-List/export-control pressure keeping Nvidia and Western equipment out. These names have *no* natural hedge if Beijing's localization push slows, and their growth cannot be cross-checked against a global AI-capex benchmark because they largely don't participate in it.
2. **Global-AI-supply-chain plays domiciled in China (Montage, Innolight, Eoptolink):** revenue tracks worldwide hyperscaler/Nvidia capex, is priced substantially in USD, and is exposed to a *different* risk: further US restrictions on components/customers reaching Chinese suppliers of US buyers, not a slowdown in Chinese localization policy. These are, counter to the "China AI stack" label, best modeled as global-AI-capex plays with China-jurisdiction/export-control tail risk layered on top.
3. **Ambiguous/transitional (NAURA, AMEC, CXMT, YMTC, Hua Hong):** directionally domestic-majority today, with small but real and rising optionality toward global demand (AMEC's claimed TSMC etch-tool qualification; CXMT's Western PC-OEM DRAM evaluations) that is unverified at the revenue-materiality level and should not be treated as a current growth driver.

### What's priced in — cross-cutting read

- **The China domestic-substitution names (SMIC, Cambricon) carry the largest gap between fundamentals and multiple** — SMIC trades at a mid-teens-to-low-hundreds P/E premium to TSMC despite a multi-year technology/yield deficit (Pod 1), and Cambricon's multiple is off the charts versus Nvidia's on almost any basis attempted. Both are best read as options on continued policy support and scarcity value (only domestic supplier of its kind), not as multiples that would survive a discounted-cash-flow test against disclosed fundamentals.
- **The global-supply-chain names (Montage, Innolight) show the opposite pattern** — growth at or above their US-listed comparators, priced at a *discount* multiple, consistent with investors demanding a China/export-control risk premium rather than doubting the growth itself.
- **Alibaba is the pod's clearest "cheap growth, if you trust the segment" case** — its AI-cloud segment growth now outpaces AWS's, at roughly half the group multiple, but the comparison is confounded by group-vs-segment accounting and known ADR/regulatory-discount dynamics documented elsewhere in this program (Pod 6).
- **CXMT/YMTC valuations are pure pre-IPO price discovery** — treat the >5x swing in CXMT's implied valuation over six months as a signal of genuine uncertainty, not as evidence for any particular number.

## GLOSSARY

- **Consensus** — the average (or median) of individual sell-side analysts' published estimates for a company's future revenue, earnings, or other metric; used as the market's collective expectation against which actual results and company guidance are compared.
- **Forward P/E** — a company's current share price divided by its *estimated* future (usually next-twelve-months or next-fiscal-year) earnings per share, as opposed to trailing P/E (based on already-reported earnings); a lower forward P/E for the same growth rate implies the market is paying less for that future growth.
- **PEG (ratio)** — forward P/E divided by the expected earnings growth rate; a rough single-number way to ask "is this multiple justified by this growth rate," with PEG near or below 1 conventionally read as not-overpriced relative to growth (not used as a hard threshold in this memo given data-quality caveats above).
- **Urban-NOA** — "urban Network-of-Autonomy," an industry term (referenced in the brief's glossary list for this pod but not substantively used in this memo's company set) for the share of an autonomous-driving supplier's deployed fleet operating without safety-driver intervention in dense urban environments; see Pod 5 for the L9/robotics names where it applies.
- **QoQ / YoY** — quarter-over-quarter / year-over-year, the two standard growth-rate comparison windows used throughout this memo; QoQ is more volatile and seasonally sensitive, YoY smooths seasonality but lags turning points.
- **Entity List** — the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) roster of foreign parties subject to specific export-license requirements for US-origin technology; a company's Entity List status is a first-order determinant of whether it can plausibly earn "global" revenue at all (see domestic/global split table above).
- **WFE** — wafer fab equipment; the category of tools (etch, deposition, cleaning, lithography, etc.) used to manufacture semiconductors, as opposed to the chips themselves.
- **HBM** — high-bandwidth memory; stacked DRAM connected via through-silicon vias, the memory type that gates AI-accelerator throughput and the subject of the CXMT/YMTC indigenization race (see Pod 2).
- **GPM** — gross profit margin.
- **STAR Market** — Shanghai Stock Exchange's Nasdaq-style board for tech/growth listings (科创板), where most of the Chinese AI-chip and memory IPOs in this dataset are listed or plan to list.
