# v2 Pod P2 — Models, Apps & Adoption Economics

**Analyst pod:** v2 P2 · **Date:** 2026-07-10 · **Horizon:** mid-2026 snapshot · **Extends:** `research/pod4_cloud_models_apps.md` (v1), read first — this memo does not repeat v1's capability/benchmark findings and instead drills into the demand-side question v1 flagged but did not answer: **is the model-lab revenue real, and how do open-weight labs actually make money?**

---

## Scope

This memo covers **L7 foundation-model-lab economics** (DeepSeek, Zhipu/Z.ai, MiniMax, Moonshot/Kimi, Alibaba/Qwen, ByteDance/Doubao, Tencent/Hunyuan, Baidu/Ernie) and **L8 enterprise-agent adoption**, with two specific mandates from the v2 brief: (1) separate *market share/token share* from *actual revenue and profit*, since v1 and most press coverage conflate the two; and (2) investigate **OpenClaw**, the viral open-source agent framework, and the Alibaba/Tencent enterprise-agent products built on it, as a test of whether China's enterprise AI-adoption curve has genuinely inflected in 2026 or is still adoption-without-monetization.

The headline finding: **2026 is the first year two Chinese model labs (Zhipu, MiniMax) have public, audited-adjacent financials** (HKEX IPO prospectuses + first post-listing annual reports), which is a genuine evidentiary upgrade — these are the only two labs in this memo where revenue figures clear the v2 A/B bar. Every other lab's revenue is D-grade estimate, and the estimates disagree with each other by up to 100x for the same company in the same year. Market-share/token-share metrics (OpenRouter share, DAU, Hugging Face downloads — all covered extensively in v1) are real and well-sourced; **revenue is not**, except at the two newly-listed names, and even there it is dwarfed by losses.

## Market structure & value chain

**The Chinese model-lab landscape splits into three revenue-visibility tiers**, and this tiering matters more than the technical-capability tiering v1 used:

1. **Publicly disclosed (B-grade, filing-based):** Zhipu AI (HKEX:2513) and MiniMax (HKEX:0100-W) are, as of January 2026, the only two Chinese foundation-model developers with prospectus-grade and post-IPO annual-report financials. This is a step-change in evidentiary quality — for the first time a reader can cite an actual income statement rather than an estimate.
2. **Indirectly disclosed (C-grade, bundled into a parent's segment reporting):** Alibaba's Qwen (via DashScope/Model Studio, folded into Alibaba Cloud revenue with no standalone Qwen P&L line), ByteDance's Doubao (via Volcengine's "MaaS" — Model-as-a-Service — revenue target disclosed to press but not itemized in a filing, since ByteDance is private and does not publish consolidated financials), Tencent's Hunyuan (folded into Tencent Cloud and reported only via pricing announcements and enterprise-integration counts, not a P&L line), and Baidu's Ernie (partially disclosed — Baidu is SEC-registered and its 6-K filings do break out "AI-related revenue" and "revenue from AI applications" as named line items, making Baidu the best-disclosed of the cloud-parented labs).
3. **Undisclosed / estimate-only (D-grade):** DeepSeek and Moonshot AI are both private, unlisted, and have published no financials. All revenue figures for these two are third-party estimates, and — critically — **the estimates for DeepSeek disagree with each other by roughly two orders of magnitude** (see below). This is the sharpest illustration in this pod of "flag hard."

**Open-source monetization reality — the core finding.** Every major Chinese lab ships open-weight (or hybrid open/closed) models, and the honest answer to "how do they make money from that" is: **mostly they don't, directly.** The mechanism in every case examined is a variant of what the industry calls **cloud pull-through**: open weights are a zero-marginal-cost customer-acquisition and mindshare tool; the actual monetizable events are (a) paid API/inference calls on the lab's or its parent's cloud, (b) on-premises/localized enterprise deployment contracts (large, low-margin, government/SOE-heavy), and (c) enterprise "agent" and support-services layers built on top of the model. Zhipu's own disclosed revenue mix is the clearest evidence available: in H1 2025, **84.8% of Zhipu's revenue came from localized (on-premises) enterprise/government deployment, only 15.2% from cloud API calls** — i.e., the open-weight-driven "many small developers pay per token" story that dominates Western open-source narratives is, for the actual revenue-leading Chinese lab, a minority of revenue; the majority is old-fashioned systems-integration-style contract revenue to government and state-linked enterprise customers (88.4% of Zhipu's localized-deployment revenue is domestic-mainland) [WEB-603, WEB-604]. By full-year 2025, Zhipu's cloud-API mix had grown to ~26% of revenue as its MaaS ARR line rose 60x (off a very small base) to RMB 1.7bn, with cloud-API gross margin improving from 3.3% to 18.9% — real but still thin, and still smaller than the enterprise-deployment segment [WEB-600, WEB-603, WEB-604].

For the cloud-parented labs (Alibaba, ByteDance, Tencent, Baidu), the mechanism is more direct: **the model is a loss-leading (or at best break-even) top-of-funnel product for the parent's cloud-infrastructure business**, monetized through GPU rental, storage, and managed-inference consumption rather than the model itself carrying a standalone margin. ByteDance is explicit about this: Volcengine's 2026 MaaS revenue target of RMB 15bn (a 10x jump from ~RMB 1.5bn in 2025) is presented by ByteDance/press as a deliberate near-term margin sacrifice to seed token consumption and developer lock-in to the Doubao model family, not as a standalone profit center [prior-cited in v1 pod4, WEB-627 confirms the "cloud commodity to currency" framing for Alibaba specifically]. Baidu is the most transparent of the cloud-parented group because it files with the SEC: in its Q1 2026 6-K, Baidu disclosed "Revenue from AI Applications" of RMB 2.5bn, described as **approximately flat year-over-year** even as GPU-cloud subscription revenue grew 184% YoY and total AI-related revenue crossed 50% of group revenue for the first time [WEB-633, WEB-634]. Read together, this is the clearest primary-source evidence in this pod of the **adoption/monetization gap**: Baidu's Ernie ecosystem has 200m+ MAU and 26,000+ active enterprise customers processing 50m+ queries/day, yet the specific "AI applications" revenue line is not growing — the growth is almost entirely in infrastructure (GPU/cloud) consumption, not in a monetizable model/app layer [WEB-634]. This is consistent with, and sharpens, v1's flagged "enterprise monetization gap."

## Capability vs. global frontier (+ timeline)

Not re-litigated here — see v1 pod4 for the single-best-model gap (6-9 months, narrowing) and token-supply gap (China ~61% of OpenRouter tokens by May 2026) findings, both of which stand. This pod's addition: **capability convergence has outpaced revenue convergence.** GLM-5.2 and Hunyuan Hy3 rank near the global frontier on benchmarks (v1, [WEB-417][WEB-418][WEB-423][WEB-424]) while their developers (Zhipu, Tencent) report either large losses (Zhipu) or no standalone model P&L at all (Tencent). Capability parity has not translated into revenue parity with OpenAI/Anthropic-scale monetization — the token-share statistic that dominates coverage is a distribution metric, not a revenue metric, and the two are only loosely correlated in the data available.

## Moats & unit economics — lab-by-lab

**DeepSeek (private).** No financials disclosed. Third-party estimates for 2025-2026 revenue span from **$13.4m** (Latka, small-team-revenue aggregator, extrapolated from headcount) to a **~$220m annual run-rate** (mid-2025, unnamed aggregator methodology) to **>$1.1bn for 2026** (businessofapps.com, method undisclosed) — a spread of roughly 80x across sources published within the same window, none of them a named primary source [WEB-609, WEB-611, WEB-612]. Separately, DeepSeek's own March-2025 technical disclosure computed a **"theoretical" 545% cost-profit margin** assuming 100% of inference had been billed at list price — the company explicitly stated actual realized revenue is "substantially lower" because most usage is discounted, subsidized, or unmonetized [WEB-609, WEB-610]. **Grade: D on every revenue figure; treat all DeepSeek revenue numbers as unverified estimates, not facts.** The one hard data point is DeepSeek's May-2026 primary funding round (~$7.4bn at $50bn-plus valuation, led by Tencent + CATL + National AI Industry Investment Fund, C-grade per v1) — a capital-raise, not a revenue disclosure, and notable precisely because DeepSeek needed *external* capital for the first time despite years of viral open-weight distribution.

**Zhipu AI / Z.ai (HKEX:2513, listed).** The single best-disclosed Chinese model lab. FY2025 (year to Dec-2025): revenue RMB 724.33m (~$104.8m), **up 131.9% YoY but missing analyst estimates**; net loss RMB 4.72bn, **up 59.5% YoY**, driven by R&D spend of RMB 3.18bn (+44.9% YoY); overall gross margin *declined* from 56.3% to 41.0% as the mix shifted toward lower-margin on-premises deployment [WEB-600, WEB-602, WEB-604]. Revenue mix: enterprise general-purpose model deployment ~RMB 3.66bn-equivalent segment description (largest, +70% YoY per aggregator breakdown), cloud-API/MaaS RMB 1.90bn (+293% YoY, now ~26% of revenue vs <16% a year prior), enterprise-agent revenue RMB 1.66bn (fastest-growing segment) [WEB-603, WEB-604] — note these three segment figures come from press synthesis of the annual report rather than a reproduced financial-statement table, so treat the *segment* breakdown as C-grade even though the *headline* revenue/loss figures are B-grade (sourced to the filed annual report). **Loss-to-revenue ratio exceeds 6:1 for FY2025** (net loss RMB 4.72bn vs revenue RMB 0.72bn); H1 2025 in isolation was worse, with a reported ~12:1 ratio [WEB-608]. Monthly cash burn estimated at ~RMB 300m [WEB-608, unnamed methodology, D]. **The market is pricing Zhipu far ahead of its fundamentals**: shares rose an estimated 1,500-2,000% YTD into a market cap that peaked above HK$1tn before a follow-on $4bn raise in July 2026 (all figures per v1 pod4, reconfirmed here) — a valuation trajectory that is essentially disconnected from the loss-making, sub-$105m-revenue business the prospectus/annual-report describes.

**MiniMax (HKEX:0100-W, listed).** FY2025 revenue $79.0m, **up 158.9% YoY**; net loss $1.87bn for the full year (loss driven substantially by valuation/share-based adjustments tied to the IPO, not purely operating cash burn) [MiniMax's own press release, WEB-605 — a genuine B-grade primary source]. Revenue mix is unusually consumer-weighted for a "frontier lab": **AI-native consumer products (Talkie companion app, Hailuo video generator) contributed ~67% of FY2025 revenue ($53.1m, +143.4% YoY)**, while the **"Open Platform" enterprise/API segment contributed ~33% ($26.0m, +197.8% YoY)** at a notably higher gross margin (~69.4% for that segment specifically) [WEB-605, WEB-606]. More than 70% of total revenue is international, not domestic — MiniMax is the one lab in this set whose revenue is predominantly *not* a domestic-substitution story. Operating burn was severe pre-IPO: 9M-2025 net loss of $512m on $53.4m revenue (~10:1 ratio); post-IPO cash position (>$1bn) is reported to support roughly four years of runway at the pre-IPO burn rate [WEB-608]. S&P Global's own forward model (a forecast, not a disclosure — C/D grade) projects MiniMax revenue reaching ~$219m in 2026 and ~$5.8bn by 2030 — an extremely aggressive extrapolation that should be read as a sell-side scenario, not guidance [WEB-607].

**Moonshot AI / Kimi (private).** No filed financials. Press reports (dated April-May 2026, sourced to unnamed company disclosures relayed via investor/press channels) put **annualized recurring revenue (ARR) crossing $100m in March 2026 and doubling to over $200m by April 2026** — a genuine growth signal if accurate, but D-grade (no named primary source, no filing) [WEB-616]. Moonshot's valuation trajectory is unusually compressed in time: ~$4.3bn end-2025 → ~$10bn (Jan-2026, $700m raise) → $20bn (May-2026, $2bn round led by Meituan's Long-Z Investments, with Tsinghua Capital/China Mobile/CPE Yuanfeng participating) → in active talks for $30bn (June-2026) [WEB-613, WEB-614, WEB-615]. The valuation-to-disclosed-revenue multiple this implies (~100-150x ARR at the $20-30bn marks, if the $200m ARR figure holds) is extreme even by frontier-AI-lab standards and should be flagged as a momentum/strategic-investor valuation rather than a revenue-multiple-justified one.

**Alibaba/Qwen, ByteDance/Doubao, Tencent/Hunyuan (cloud-parented, no standalone model P&L).** None of the three discloses model-level revenue; all three monetize primarily through the parent cloud business (see Market structure section above). The clearest quantified enterprise-traction data point among them is Tencent's, where **more than 150 mainland Chinese enterprises had integrated Tencent Hunyuan 3D via Tencent Cloud** as of mid-2026 (named examples: Unity China, Bambu Lab, Liblib) [Tencent official source, referenced via search — B-grade primary]. Pricing power is real and recently exercised: Tencent's Hunyuan API list prices rose as much as 463% off promotional rates in March 2026 (part of the broader 2026 industry-wide compute-price-hike cycle documented in v1), which is evidence of tightening margins/scarcity pricing rather than of underlying model-level profitability.

**Baidu/Ernie (SEC-registered, partial disclosure).** See Market structure above — RMB 2.5bn "AI Applications" revenue, ~flat YoY in Q1 2026, is the cleanest single monetization-gap data point in this memo precisely because it comes from an actual 6-K filing rather than a press estimate [WEB-633].

## Enterprise-adoption shift & OpenClaw

**What OpenClaw is.** OpenClaw is a free, MIT-licensed, self-hosted "personal AI agent" framework — an orchestration layer that lets an LLM autonomously operate a computer, call arbitrary tools/APIs, and act across messaging platforms as its primary interface. It was created by Peter Steinberger (an Austrian developer, founder of the PSPDFKit document-SDK company) and first published in **November 2025**; it was renamed twice in quick succession (initially "Clawdbot," then briefly "Moltbot" in late January 2026 amid a reported trademark dispute, then "OpenClaw" days later) before going viral in February-April 2026, reportedly surpassing **250,000-350,000 GitHub stars** and becoming, per several tech-press accounts, one of the fastest-growing open-source repositories on record [WEB-617 (Exponential View), WEB-618, WEB-619 (Wikipedia, tertiary), WEB-620]. Steinberger subsequently announced he would join OpenAI and hand stewardship to an independent "OpenClaw Foundation" [WEB-620]. **None of this is Chinese in origin** — it is important to be precise that OpenClaw itself is a Western open-source project; the China story is entirely about what Chinese platform companies built *on top of* it in the weeks after it went viral.

**What Alibaba and Tencent built on it.** Within roughly a five-week window in March 2026, three of China's largest platform companies shipped OpenClaw-derived or OpenClaw-compatible enterprise-agent products:
- **Tencent** shipped the widest suite: **WorkBuddy** (a workplace agent, piloted with 2,000+ non-technical employees across HR/admin/ops before general release), **ClawBot** (an OpenClaw-based agent surfaced as a WeChat contact, giving WeChat's 1bn+ MAU base a chat-native path to agent automation), and **ClawPro** (an enterprise agent-management platform in public beta, letting businesses deploy/govern agents with token-consumption tracking and security controls; **more than 200 organizations across finance, government, and manufacturing used ClawPro during its internal beta**) [WEB-621, WEB-622, WEB-623]. Tencent Cloud subsequently pushed WorkBuddy to a global audience after the domestic launch [WEB-621].
- **Alibaba** launched **Wukong** (March 17, 2026), a multi-agent enterprise-coordination platform built by Alibaba's DingTalk unit and running on the Qwen model family rather than any Western model — explicitly positioned as reaching **DingTalk's existing base of ~20 million organizations** without a new install, with e-commerce, manufacturing, finance, legal, recruitment, and content-creation workflow templates, and stated plans to extend integration to Slack, Microsoft Teams, and WeChat [WEB-625, WEB-626, WEB-627].
- **Baidu and ByteDance** also released agent products in the same window per press coverage, though with less-detailed enterprise-traction disclosure than Tencent's or Alibaba's [WEB-624].

**Has enterprise adoption inflected?** The directional evidence says yes, but the magnitude and monetization are still thin and mostly self-reported by the vendors:
- A Chinese-enterprise survey cited across multiple 2026 sources found the share of Chinese **industrial** enterprises applying large models/AI agents rose from **9.6% (2024) to 47.5% (2025)**, with multi-function application rising from 1.7% to 35% — a genuine inflection in *stated* adoption, though the underlying survey methodology/sample was not independently verifiable in this research pass (C-grade, industry-tracker sourced) [prior-cited in v1, reconfirmed WEB-628].
- The Deloitte-China/HKU **AI Adoption Index 2026**, based on a survey of 100+ Chinese C-suite leaders, is a named, dated, credible-institution survey (B-grade as a primary survey instrument, though its own N is small) and explicitly frames 2026 China enterprise AI as **"the paradox of promise and performance"** — i.e., adoption/intent is high but measured business outcomes lag [WEB-630].
- State-directed adoption (SOE pilot cohorts — Baosteel, XCMG, Haier; 15 "pioneer" smart factories with >70% AI scenario coverage, 6,000+ deployed models) remains policy-target-driven rather than market-pull-driven, as flagged in v1 [reconfirmed WEB-628].
- The clearest **quantified enterprise-agent commercial traction** figures in this memo are Tencent's 200+ ClawPro beta organizations and Baidu's 26,000+ active Ernie enterprise customers — both real, both modest relative to the country's enterprise base, and neither accompanied by disclosed agent-specific revenue.

**Net read:** 2026 shows a genuine adoption inflection in stated/piloted usage (survey data, beta-org counts, MAU/DAU) triggered in part by the OpenClaw viral moment giving Chinese platforms a proven, low-risk-to-copy product template — but there is **no comparable inflection yet in disclosed agent-specific revenue**. The Baidu "AI Applications revenue flat YoY despite 200m+ MAU" data point is the single most concrete piece of evidence that adoption is currently ahead of monetization, consistent with v1's flagged gap and with the Deloitte-HKU survey's own framing.

## Bottlenecks & dependencies

1. **Revenue opacity is itself a structural bottleneck for investors.** Five of the eight names in this memo (DeepSeek, Moonshot, plus the *model-level* economics of Alibaba/ByteDance/Tencent's in-house labs) have no disclosed, filing-based revenue at all. An investor cannot underwrite "the model business" at Alibaba, ByteDance, or Tencent separately from the cloud business it is bundled into.
2. **Loss-to-revenue ratios at the two listed, disclosed labs are extreme** (Zhipu ~6:1 FY2025, worse intra-year; MiniMax ~10:1 pre-IPO) — open-weighting has bought distribution and valuation momentum, not profitability, and both companies' own filings describe gross-margin compression as deployment mix shifts toward lower-margin, government/SOE-heavy on-premises contracts.
3. **The DeepSeek revenue-estimate spread (roughly 80-100x across sources) is a case study in why D-grade figures should not be treated as facts** — this is the sharpest single illustration in the P2 mandate of "market share ≠ revenue," since DeepSeek's OpenRouter token share (largest of any single provider globally, per v1) coexists with total revenue uncertainty spanning $13m to >$1bn.
4. **OpenClaw's open, MIT-licensed nature is a double-edged commercial dependency for the Chinese platforms building on it**: it let Tencent and Alibaba ship credible enterprise-agent products in weeks rather than years, but the same openness means the underlying framework itself is not a moat — any competitor (domestic or foreign) can fork it, and stewardship now sits with an independent foundation outside any single company's control.
5. **Adoption/monetization gap remains the central unresolved risk into 2027**, per both this memo's Baidu data point and the Deloitte-HKU survey framing — policy targets (70% agent adoption by 2027) are diffusion mandates, not revenue guarantees.

## Investability read

- **Zhipu and MiniMax are the only pure-play, filing-backed model-lab equities that exist anywhere globally** — a genuinely new asset class — but both are currently priced far ahead of fundamentals (loss-to-revenue ratios of 6:1 and 10:1 respectively) on what looks like momentum/scarcity-value trading (first-mover "AI tiger IPO" status) rather than discounted-cash-flow logic. Treat as high-beta satellite positions, not value plays, per v1's read — this pod's financial detail reinforces rather than revises that call.
- **DeepSeek and Moonshot offer no direct equity access** and their revenue cannot be independently verified — any exposure is indirect (Tencent/CATL balance-sheet exposure to DeepSeek via the 2026 funding round; Meituan/Tsinghua Capital/China Mobile exposure to Moonshot).
- **Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu remain the cleanest listed exposure to the model layer**, precisely because the model business is *not* separable from — and is subsidized by — their disclosed, profitable cloud/ads/e-commerce businesses; an investor is buying the platform, not the model economics specifically. Baidu's SEC filings currently offer the most granular (if still partial) look at where AI-applications revenue actually sits relative to infrastructure revenue.
- **OpenClaw itself is not investable** (open-source, foundation-stewarded); the investable read is indirect, via which platform (Tencent, Alibaba) converts the OpenClaw-driven product cycle into disclosed enterprise-agent revenue first — nothing in the record as of July 2026 shows that conversion yet.

## GLOSSARY

- **Open-weight (open-source model):** a model whose trained parameters ("weights") are published for anyone to download, run, and fine-tune, as opposed to a "closed" model accessible only via a paid API.
- **MaaS (Model-as-a-Service):** a cloud-provider business line that sells access to AI models (via API calls, fine-tuning, or hosted inference) as a metered service, analogous to "Software-as-a-Service" but for models specifically.
- **Cloud pull-through:** the practice of giving away a model (or software) for free/cheap to drive paying customers into a related, monetized infrastructure product (here: GPU rental, storage, managed inference) — the model is the marketing funnel, the cloud is the revenue.
- **Agentic (AI agent):** an AI system that can autonomously plan and execute multi-step tasks — calling tools, browsing, operating software — rather than just answering a single prompt.
- **Inference vs. training:** "training" is the one-time (or periodic) compute-intensive process of building a model from data; "inference" is the repeated, ongoing process of running the trained model to answer real user queries — inference is the cost base that scales with usage/revenue.
- **Token:** the basic unit of text an LLM processes and generates (roughly ¾ of a word in English); API pricing and most revenue/usage metrics in this memo are denominated in $-per-million-tokens or tokens-processed.
- **ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue):** an annualized run-rate of subscription/repeat revenue, commonly used by private, unlisted companies as a forward-looking proxy for revenue since they do not file periodic financials — inherently an estimate/extrapolation, not an audited figure.
- **6-K:** a form foreign private issuers (like Baidu) file with the U.S. SEC to furnish material information, including quarterly results — a primary, checkable regulatory disclosure.
- **WVR (Weighted Voting Rights) structure:** a dual-class share structure (used by MiniMax's HKEX listing) that lets founders retain outsized voting control relative to their economic ownership stake.
